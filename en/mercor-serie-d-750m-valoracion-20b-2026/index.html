Mercor raised $750M at a $20 billion valuation: AI money is moving to human evaluation

By inicIA · November 13, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 10, 2026, Mercor announced a $750 million Series D at a $20 billion valuation. The round includes an employee tender, and Mercor does not say how much of the $750 million is new capital for the company and how much buys shares from staff. Named investors include Benchmark, Felicis, General Catalyst, NVIDIA, Salesforce and Zoom, among others. The valuation is double the $10 billion it reached after its $350 million Series C in October 2025, according to Reuters. The money will expand evaluation, post-training and simulated work environments for enterprise AI agents.

What Mercor does and why it is worth $20 billion

  • It connects labs with human experts: Mercor, founded in 2023 in San Francisco, pays specialists to build the data and tests labs use to train and measure their models.
  • The thesis is evaluation by occupation: chief executive Brendan Foody says every occupation in the economy needs its own agents, and that in the United States alone there are more than 800 distinct occupations with workflows models have not been evaluated against.
  • NVIDIA moves from customer to shareholder: the chipmaker already bought expert data from Mercor for its Nemotron models, and now joins as an investor, alongside Salesforce and Zoom.
  • The round names no lead investor: the company lists 13 participants and names no lead. Mercor is private and does not publish audited financial statements.

What changes for your business

  • Evaluation is the new bottleneck: models get cheaper and multiply, and what is scarce is proof that an agent does one specific job well. That proof comes from expert people, with data and simulated environments.
  • The enterprise agent is measured by occupation: sales, support, accounting and logistics are different jobs, and each needs its own evaluation. Evaluation by occupation is what answers concrete work.
  • Money follows agent infrastructure: a $20 billion valuation says investors see evaluation as infrastructure, carrying the same weight as compute and models.
  • The revenue figures are company claims: Foody said on X that run-rate revenue went from $1 million to $3 billion in 27 months. Mercor publishes no audited statements, so the figure comes from its co-founder and awaits independent verification.

What to do in your business this week

  • Write the evaluation before buying the agent: define 10 real tasks from your business, with the expected result and the pass criterion. Only the agent that passes your test gets in; the demo only opens the door.
  • Ask the vendor for its evaluation in writing: which tasks the agent was measured on, with which data and which success rate. The evaluation sheet is part of the contract.
  • Measure your agent on real work every month: evaluation is a monthly habit. The agent measured every month improves; monthly measurement is what sustains quality.
  • Budget evaluation as a fixed cost: data, tests and human review are part of the agent's cost, with their own line in the budget, as one more fixed cost of the agent.
