ChatGPT Wallet: OpenAI agents can now pay for you, with your approval on every purchase

By inicIA · November 15, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 13, 2026, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, a wallet that lets its personal agents, called dots, complete purchases for the user. The launch starts as a limited rollout for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States, aged 18 and older, with eligible U.S.-issued cards. Every purchase goes through the user's review and approval with a security check, such as Face ID or a fingerprint. The real card stays out of reach of the agent and the merchant: the card network issues a single-use payment token for that purchase. OpenAI built Wallet together with American Express, Mastercard and Visa.

How a Wallet purchase works

  • The user registers the card once: the eligible card stays saved in Wallet. The agent works from there without seeing the real card number.
  • The dot finds and prepares the order: the agent finds the product, fills the cart and prepares the order on the merchant's site, on the checkout the business already has.
  • The user reviews and approves every purchase: before any charge, the person sees the items, the total and the selected card, and authorizes that specific purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint. The approval is recorded.
  • The network issues a single-use token: after approval, the card network issues a token that stands in for the real card details for that transaction. The token covers one purchase only.
  • Card benefits stay in place: OpenAI says card points, rewards and protections, including the right to dispute a charge, remain in effect. The transaction runs directly between the user and the merchant.

What changes for your business

  • The agent customer already exists: until now, the AI agent recommended and prepared the purchase, and the person paid. Wallet closes the loop: the agent completes the payment with the customer's approval. A business that sells online now serves a new buyer, the agent with a wallet.
  • Your checkout decides whether the sale closes: OpenAI says some sites block agent purchases unintentionally, through firewalls or content delivery network settings. OpenAI's merchant payments guide says Wallet works with the checkout pages and payment forms the business already has, with no Wallet-specific integration required.
  • Spending controls come later: at launch, every purchase requires human approval. OpenAI plans to add spending limits and rules by category or time period, so some purchases can be pre-approved within limits the user sets. The date for those controls is still pending.
  • OpenAI takes no cut at launch: according to CNBC, OpenAI said it takes no percentage of transactions at the start. Wallet itself carries no separate fee announced by OpenAI.

What to check this week

  • Test your own checkout as an agent would: ask your team to walk through a full purchase from an agent browser and note where it stops. An accidental block is a lost sale that your analytics records as cart abandonment.
  • Review your bot and firewall rules: if your site separates human traffic from automated traffic, write a clear rule for customer-authorized purchase agents. The line between an authorized agent and a malicious bot lives in your configuration, and it pays to write it before the traffic arrives.
  • Prepare your returns policy for agent purchases: the customer approves the purchase, and disputes follow the card's channel. Your return and refund process serves a purchase made by an agent the same way it serves one made by hand.
  • Watch the rollout before investing: Wallet starts only for Pro in the United States, through dots. Real agent-purchase volume will be measured over the coming months. Reviewing your checkout costs one review; a new integration waits until volume justifies the spend.

Note: this article summarizes OpenAI's announcement of November 13, 2026, "Introducing ChatGPT Wallet", its merchant payments guide, and coverage by The Next Web, CNBC, The Verge and TechCrunch. Dots were introduced at OpenAI's DevDay on September 29, 2026. Availability is limited to Pro subscribers in the United States; expansion to other plans and countries is still pending announcement.
