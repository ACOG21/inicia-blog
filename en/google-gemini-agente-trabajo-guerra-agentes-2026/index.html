Google Launched Its AI Agent for Work: The Agent War Is Now Official | inicIA

By inicIA · October 9, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

On October 8, 2026, Google Cloud unveiled Gemini agent: an AI agent that works autonomously inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Calendar, and also connects to Microsoft 365 and Slack. It plans the work, uses tools, connects to your company's business systems, and returns finished work in documents, emails, and developer environments. The most market-revealing technical detail: it picks the best model for each task, and today it runs on Google's Gemini models and Anthropic's Claude models. With this launch, the enterprise agent war is now official: OpenAI launched Dots in September, Meta launched Muse in September, and Microsoft revamped its agentic offering last month. For your business, this is good news: competition among giants is your pricing and feature leverage.

What Gemini agent actually does

Per Google's announcement, the agent can answer questions, handle tasks, create content, and write code. It works as a personal assistant or as a team member: working on behalf of a group (like a project manager) or on behalf of a specific role (like a finance department analyst). Two details that matter for a business:

  1. "Coworker agents" with their own identity. You can create coworker agents that act as team members, with their own email address and access only to the information they are assigned. This is exactly the model we recommend: an agent with a role, limits, and clear permissions — not a loose chatbot.
  2. Industry-specific versions. Versions for financial services and legal work are already in preview; government, healthcare, and retail are on the way. AI is no longer generic: it specializes by industry, and yours is coming soon.

The adoption figure Google shared: nearly 80% of its Google Cloud customers already use its AI products, and nearly 90% of Fortune 100 companies use Gemini Enterprise.

The war map (and why it benefits you)

In one month, the board looks like this: OpenAI with Dots (always-on agents chasing goals across apps), Meta with Muse (a personal agent that shops, books travel, sends emails, and makes payments), Microsoft with its revamped offering, and now Google with Gemini agent. Wall Street's reaction was muted (Alphabet rose just 0.2%), because the market expected it. The read for you is different: when four giants fight over the same contract, the buyer rules. Features that are premium today will be standard in six months, and prices will keep falling.

The 3 questions before adopting an enterprise agent

  1. Which systems does it touch? An agent that reads your email, calendar, and documents needs written, auditable permissions. Define what each agent can see before turning it on.
  2. Which model does it use per task? An agent picking the best model per task (as Gemini does) is good for quality and cost — but ask your vendor: who decides, and how is that decision audited?
  3. Where does your data land? An agent working across Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and Slack moves your information between clouds. Demand each integration's data-retention and usage policy in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Does Gemini agent replace my employees?

No: it works as an assistant or as a team member with a defined role. The value is in executing repetitive work across apps, freeing your people for work that requires judgment.

Does it work if my company does not use Google Workspace?

Yes: it also connects to Microsoft 365 and Slack, and to the company's business systems. Interoperability is precisely this war's battlefield.

Should I wait for prices to drop?

Do not wait: start with a bounded use case (one role, one flow) and negotiate with vendor competition in your favor. The cost of waiting is continuing to operate without agents while your competitors do.

What is a "coworker agent"?

An agent with its own identity inside your team: an email address, access limited to the information it is assigned, and a defined role. It is the most orderly model for introducing AI into a company.
