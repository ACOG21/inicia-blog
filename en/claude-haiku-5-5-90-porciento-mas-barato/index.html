Claude Haiku 5.5 Costs Up to 90% Less: The AI Price War Continues (and Benefits You) | inicIA

By inicIA · October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 7, 2026, Anthropic launched Claude Haiku 5.5: its small model now costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens on prompts under 100,000 tokens — about 75% less than Haiku 4.5 on average and up to 90% less on the most common short-prompt band — exactly matching GPT-6 Luna's price at that tier. It also adds adjustable effort levels (Low through Max), a 1-million-token context window, and stronger scores on computer-use and agent benchmarks. The message for your business: the AI price war has not stopped, and every round makes 24/7 agents cheaper.

What it brings besides the price

  • Adjustable effort, a first for the Haiku line: choose how much "thinking" the model devotes to each task, paying only for what is needed.
  • 1M-token context: full documents, histories, and knowledge bases in a single call.
  • Stronger agent and computer-use benchmarks than its predecessor: not just cheaper, but more capable at autonomous work.
  • Sonnet 5.5 got cheaper too: a 50% cut to cache reads, plus monthly API credits ($100 to $500 depending on plan) for Max and Team subscribers.

The technical community's reception was positive, with one practical warning: there is a pricing cliff above 100K tokens (up to 5x) that complicates cost planning if your prompts run long. And one concrete real-world data point: Plotly's benchmark team reported its data-analytics score jumped two letter grades at 9x lower cost.

Why this changes your agents' economics

Small models are the invisible engine of agents: message classification, lead routing, summaries, and the sub-agent work a large agent delegates. When each call costs cents per million tokens, you can:

  1. Chain calls without fearing the bill: an agent that queries, verifies, and summarizes in multiple steps stops being a luxury.
  2. Leave agents always on: inbox monitoring, continuous lead scoring, automatic follow-up.
  3. Reserve expensive models for what deserves them: the frontier model decides; the cheap model executes.

The competitive pressure is clear: Anthropic matched GPT-6 Luna's price dollar for dollar. When labs fight over your business with prices, the buyer wins.

What to do this week

  1. Audit your AI bill: which tasks today use an expensive model that a Haiku 5.5 could handle? Classification, extraction, and summarization are the first candidates.
  2. Design in two tiers: powerful model to reason, cheap model to execute at volume.
  3. Watch the 100K-token threshold: if your prompts are routinely long, that price jump can eat the savings.

Every round of this price war makes profitable a use case that didn't pencil out yesterday. The question is no longer whether you can afford an agent: it is how many you should switch on.

How much does Claude Haiku 5.5 cost?

$0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens on requests under 100,000 tokens. That is about 75% cheaper than Haiku 4.5 on average and up to 90% cheaper on the most common short-prompt band.

What are adjustable effort levels?

A first for the Haiku line: you can choose how much reasoning capacity the model devotes to each task (Low through Max), paying only for what each job needs.

Does this affect the prices of agents I buy?
