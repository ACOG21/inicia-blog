Oracle Put Agents Inside the ERP: Your Agents Will Live in Your System, Not in an App | inicIA

By inicIA · October 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On September 29, 2026, Oracle announced at an Austin event Fusion Claw: a governed agentic runtime built directly into its cloud enterprise applications. The launch adds 25 new agentic applications (Oracle's total portfolio reaches 75) across finance, human resources, supply chain and customer experience. The idea is radical and simple: AI reasons (with models like Gemini and OpenAI), but deterministic enterprise computation executes — with permissions, approval limits and auditing. Oracle shares rose about 4% on the news. The strategic signal: production agents will live inside your ERP, not in a separate app.

How it works (in business language)

Fusion Claw separates two jobs that mix badly today:

  • Reasoning: the AI model analyzes, plans and simulates (deep research, planning, modeling).
  • Executing: the enterprise's deterministic logic does the real work — ledger automation, accounting exceptions, workforce staffing, shipping consolidation, sales territory planning — with full traceability.

It operates inside your company's boundaries: role-based access control, data residency and compliance limits (including SOX), without exporting context to third parties. It integrates via APIs and agent protocols (MCP, Agent2Agent), and is configured from Oracle's AI Agent Studio.

An honest note from the auditor

The release says 25 new applications available. Independent analysts (Greyhound Research) found Oracle's public documentation named 15 capabilities as of October 6 — fewer than the release, more than nothing. This is normal in enterprise launches: marketing runs faster than documentation. The direction is real; the exact inventory is under construction. When a vendor promises you N agents, ask for the list with names.

What it means for your business (even if you are not an Oracle customer)

  1. Governance is the product: permissions, approval limits and audit receipts are what turn a chatbot into a production tool. Demand that in any implementation.
  2. The agent lives where the data lives: your ERP/CRM is the natural home of agents, not the floating chat. Plan integration there.
  3. Reason expensive, execute cheap: the winning architecture separates thinking (powerful models) from execution (deterministic, auditable computation). Ask your vendor to show you that separation.
  4. Standalone middleware gets compressed: with the big vendors absorbing orchestration, avoid tying yourself to intermediate layers with no future.
