Google Launched the Universal Gemini Work Agent: The Digital Coworker With Its Own Email | inicIA

By inicIA · October 15, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 8, 2026, at the Gemini at Work 2026 conference, Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian introduced the universal Gemini agent: a single AI agent for work that answers questions, handles knowledge work, creates images and media, and writes and runs code — all from one prompt window. The difference from a traditional assistant: it takes objectives, not instructions. It plans the work, picks tools and skills, connects to company systems, and returns finished work inside Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Chat, Calendar, Microsoft 365, and Slack. It runs persistently in the cloud: a job lasting hours or days keeps going even after you close your laptop.

What it includes, in facts

  • One agent, many surfaces: web, iOS, Android, Windows, Mac, and command line; inside Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack, and as a headless agent embedded in third-party apps.
  • Coworker agents with their own identity: describe a role and Gemini creates an agent with its own Workspace account — email, calendar, Drive, and a seat in the directory. Colleagues add it to a chat, mention it, or tag it in a document.
  • Four memory types: session, semantic, procedural, and episodic, plus a shared personalization graph.
  • Temporary sub-agents for multi-step jobs, each with its own identity.
  • Tools registry: Salesforce, ServiceNow, Jira, Snowflake, Databricks, Microsoft Office, Teams, and any MCP server; plus a registry of reusable skills.
  • Flexible models: it picks between Google's Gemini models and Anthropic's Claude models per task, balancing performance and cost.

Governance: the real announcement

Google shipped the agent with enterprise-grade controls: cryptographically attested agent identity, OAuth-propagated permissions, per-agent audit trails (every action recorded under the agent's name, not a person's), an Agent Sandbox for isolated execution, and Agent Gateway, an "AI firewall" enforcing corporate policy in real time. On costs: smart routing across models and real-time per-project spend caps that pause the agent automatically. On data: Knowledge Catalog (business definitions), Smart Storage, and a Borderless Lakehouse querying Amazon S3 and Azure Data Lake with no variable egress fees.

What it means for your business

  1. The agent becomes a team member with credentials: that demands the same rigor as a new hire — minimum access, permission reviews, audit — from day one.
  2. Objectives replace prompts: work starts by defining the expected outcome and the rules, not by writing step-by-step instructions.
  3. Cost is controlled per project: real-time spend caps are the practice you should demand of any agentic implementation, built or bought.
  4. Businesses first, consumers later: Google said the initial focus is business. If your company already lives in Workspace or Microsoft 365, this is the lane automation will arrive through.

Early testers include On, Shopify, and PayPal; Gemini Enterprise customers already include BNP Paribas, Bradesco, Merck, Orange Spain, Santee Cooper, SOMPO, Ulta Beauty, and Wesfarmers. Vertical specializations are in preview for financial services and legal (government, healthcare, and retail come next).
