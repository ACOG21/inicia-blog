Anthropic added $17 billion in one quarter and committed $895 billion to compute: what its November 2 amendment says

By inicIA · November 5, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 2, 2026, Anthropic filed Amendment No. 1 to its Form S-1, adding third-quarter 2026 results to its IPO prospectus: $16.956 billion in quarterly revenue, against about $1.4 billion a year earlier, and compute purchase commitments of about $895.4 billion. The company applied to list its Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT. The offering price and share count remain blank in the filing: the $1.5 to $2 trillion valuation, the raise of up to $100 billion and the mid-November debut are reported by media, not confirmed by the company.

The numbers that matter

  • $16.956 billion in the third quarter: revenue for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, with a net loss of $23.291 billion and an operating loss of $567 million, per the amendment.
  • $33.242 billion in nine months: 2026 revenue through September, against a second-quarter net loss of $66.309 billion.
  • $895.4 billion committed to compute: compute purchase commitments as of and after the report date, with about $70 billion payable before September 2027.
  • 93 pages of risk in 335: the prospectus devotes 93 pages to risk factors, warning that advanced AI could be weaponized, act without human oversight, or pose existential risks.
  • First half of 2026: $16.3 billion in revenue and a net loss of $80.7 billion, per Reuters' review of the original prospectus.

What to read between the filing and the reports

  • The filing says what is confirmed; reports say what is possible: the S-1 and its amendment are the source for revenue, losses and commitments. The listing date, valuation and raise size exist only in WSJ, Reuters and IFR reporting.
  • Growth is cooling in percentage terms: Reuters reported on October 15 that some investors discussed a valuation near $1.5 trillion, down from $2 trillion in September, because quarterly growth fell from about 147% to about 45%.
  • The $895 billion is the cost of leadership: the figure shows that sustaining a frontier model demands huge, long-term compute commitments, with obsolescence risk for whoever signs them.
  • The listing sets your vendor's price: if Anthropic lists in mid-November, its pressure for revenue and margins becomes public. That affects the future price of Claude and the stability of your cost per task.

Note: this article summarizes Anthropic's November 2, 2026 amendment to its Form S-1 (SEC, accession 0001628280-26-068676) and coverage by Reuters, WSJ, IFR and AFP. Revenue, loss and commitment figures come from the filed document; the listing date, valuation and raise size are reported and can change. It is not investment advice.
