Mintive: every agent with its own credential — 2.5 million calls a day managed with identity, not trust

By inicIA · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 29, 2026, Mintive launched publicly with $11 million raised in total, including a new $7 million round led by Cowboy Ventures, with participation from Coatue, Operator Collective, Burst Capital, Maven Ventures and Hustle Fund. Per the company, its platform processes 2.5 million agent calls a day across more than 50 paying enterprise customers, and has handled more than 200 million calls in seven months. Its central idea is simple: treat each agent the way an enterprise treats an employee, with its own identity, only the access its work requires, and a record of everything it does.

The platform's five pieces

  • Agent Identity: each agent gets its own scoped, revocable credential, delegated from the person it works for.
  • MCP Gateway: governs tool access, so one set of rules applies across agents, models and tools.
  • LLM Gateway: governs AI model calls under the same rules.
  • Mint Guard: screens calls for prompt injection, exposed secrets and personal data before they reach enterprise systems.
  • Monitoring and coworker agents: each tool call can be traced to the initiating person, the acting agent, the server and the model. The platform is SOC 2 Type II audited, per the company.

Why agent identity became infrastructure

  • The scale the company cites: Mintive cites Gartner: by the end of 2026, 40% of enterprise applications will feature task-specific AI agents, up from less than 5% a year earlier; and by 2028, the average Fortune 500 enterprise will have over 150,000 agents, up from fewer than 15 in 2025. These are Gartner projections cited in the announcement, not Mintive measurements.
  • Named customers: Ro in healthcare, Modern Treasury in payments infrastructure, Coursera and the University of Michigan in education, Apax in private equity, plus Braze and Stability AI per the company's blog. These are company-named customers, not an audited list.
  • The round is small on purpose: coverage notes Mintive raised only $11 million in total while rivals chase unicorn valuations. The company's reading is that agent infrastructure is won with customers in production, not with large rounds.
  • The question for your business: if you deploy 50 agents tomorrow, can you revoke one agent's access without touching the others? If the answer requires redoing shared credentials, your problem is identity, not models.
