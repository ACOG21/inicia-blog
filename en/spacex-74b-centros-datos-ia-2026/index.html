SpaceX pledges $74 billion for two AI campuses: a real pledge, a pending permit

By inicIA · November 14, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 12, 2026, SpaceX announced it will invest more than $74 billion in two AI supercomputing campuses: about $50 billion in Cochise County, Arizona, with up to 6 gigawatts of power demand, and more than $24 billion at Starbase, Texas, with its own gas-fired power plant. Neither project is approved: both go to a vote on December 9, the Arizona one before the county planning commission and the Texas one before the city commission over a tax abatement. SpaceX named no chip supplier for either campus.

The two campuses, figure by figure

  • Cochise, Arizona, about $50 billion: about 747 acres, up to nine computing buildings, up to 6 gigawatts at full build in four 1.5-gigawatt phases. SpaceX talks about roughly 5,500 construction jobs and 1,000 permanent jobs. Special-use application SU26-22 goes to a vote on December 9, and a petition with 2,400 signatures asks the county to deny the permit.
  • Starbase, Texas, more than $24 billion: 444 acres beside Starbase, where SpaceX will build, own and operate its own gas-fired power plant to generate the campus's electricity. The project has already generated more than 3,000 construction jobs and would add more than 500 permanent jobs once operating.
  • The big customer is Anthropic: Bloomberg reports Anthropic is SpaceX's largest customer. Anthropic's S-1 amendment of November 10 lists computing agreements with SpaceXAI worth up to $120.5 billion through 2029, cancellable on 90 days' notice after the initial non-cancelable periods of January and March 2027. The figure is a ceiling, and cancellation is an option.
  • SpaceX already spends at that pace: the company reported $48.1 billion of capital spending in the first nine months of 2026. The $74 billion equals about 14 months of that spending pace, on Live in the Future's arithmetic from SpaceX's figures.

What changes for your business

  • Power is the AI bottleneck: 6 gigawatts in a single rural county is the scale of the problem. Compute capacity is announced in dollars; power is approved in local votes, permits and deals with the state regulator.
  • The pledge and the permit are two different things: the $74 billion is SpaceX's plan, and the permit arrives on December 9 in two separate votes. A plan without a permit is an intention, and December 9 is the date that rules the calendar.
  • The big customer is a lab going public: Anthropic's agreements with SpaceXAI worth up to $120.5 billion show how labs tie themselves to capacity. Cancellation on 90 days' notice shows the commitment has an exit.
  • The chip is the missing fact: SpaceX named no chip supplier. The public reading points to Nvidia, which powers most AI supercomputing campuses, and Jensen Huang's resignation on November 12 puts that reading at the center of the week.

What to do in your business this week

  • Write December 9 in your calendar: the two votes that day decide whether the $74 billion moves forward. The Cochise vote and the Starbase vote are the next chapter of this story.
  • Review your compute contract: if your supplier promises new capacity, ask for the permit date and the delivery date. Announced capacity without a permit is a pledge; capacity with a permit is a plan.
  • Ask about your supplier's power: power is the cost that rises and the permit that takes time. Your supplier owes you an answer on where its electricity comes from and what happens if the permit slips.
  • Read this article next to the Nvidia one: Jensen Huang's resignation and the SpaceX announcement landed on the same day. The chip, the power and the lab are three pieces of the same board, and each one moves on its own.

Investment, jobs and power figures come from SpaceX's statement. Frequently asked questions

How much will SpaceX invest in its two AI campuses?

More than $74 billion, under its November 12, 2026 announcement: about $50 billion in Cochise County, Arizona, and more than $24 billion at Starbase, Texas.

Are SpaceX's campuses approved?

Neither one. The Arizona campus needs a county special-use permit from Cochise County and the Texas one a city tax abatement from Starbase. Both go to a vote on December 9, 2026.

How much power will the Arizona campus use?

Up to 6 gigawatts at full build, in four 1.5-gigawatt phases, across about 747 acres with up to nine computing buildings. That is the project's maximum figure, under the county application.

Who is the big customer for SpaceX's campuses?

Bloomberg reports Anthropic is the largest customer. Anthropic's S-1 amendment of November 10 lists computing agreements with SpaceXAI worth up to $120.5 billion through 2029, cancellable on 90 days' notice.
