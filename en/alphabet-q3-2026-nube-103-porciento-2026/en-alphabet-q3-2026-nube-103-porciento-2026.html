Alphabet won the cloud with AI: Google Cloud doubled to $30.7B in Q3 2026 while spending $51.4B

By inicIA · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 28, 2026, Alphabet reported third-quarter revenue of $127.6 billion, up 25% year over year. The engine was Google Cloud, at $30.7 billion, up 103%, driven by demand for AI infrastructure, AI solutions and core cloud services, with a significant contribution from TPU system sales, per the company. Cloud operating income rose to $11.1 billion, from $3.6 billion a year earlier. The other side of the report: capital spending for the quarter was $51.4 billion and free cash flow was -$9.1 billion, the second consecutive negative quarter.

The breakdown that matters

  • Google Services: $96.3 billion, up 11%. Within that segment, Search and other revenue totaled $63.4 billion (+12%), YouTube advertising $11.2 billion, and subscriptions, platforms and devices $14.3 billion.
  • Total operating income: $40.6 billion, up 30%. Diluted earnings per share came to $2.12, after an $8.7 billion loss on equity securities that reduced EPS by $0.55. That loss is from investments, not from the operating business.
  • The backlog: Google Cloud's backlog reached $561 billion in the quarter, per the earnings-call transcript. Most of it relates to typical cloud contracts, not a single customer.
  • Gemini usage: CEO Sundar Pichai said the Gemini app reached 1 billion users and that first-party model APIs process nearly 32 billion tokens per minute. Those are company-reported figures, not independent measures of efficiency.

Why the stock fell even as the cloud doubled

  • Total revenue landed just below expectations: $127.6 billion against a market expectation near $128.2 billion, a 0.49% gap. The market punishes the detail even when growth is 25%.
  • Spending scares more than growth: $51.4 billion in capital spending in one quarter, more than double the $24.0 billion of the same quarter a year earlier, with free cash flow negative for a second straight quarter. Alphabet also issued $28.7 billion in senior notes and about $12.4 billion in stock during the quarter.
  • Reading the stock move: shares fell about 8% in after-hours trading, to near $298, per TradingKey. A post-close market move is a reaction, not a verdict on the business.
  • The open question: whether AI infrastructure demand sustains this pace when capital spending normalizes. The report shows real demand and real cost, on the same page.

What it means for your business

  • AI cloud is already the business: when a provider doubles its cloud in a year on AI demand, the discussion stops being whether AI is used in production. It becomes how much it costs to run, and who pays.
  • Your bill follows the same logic: capacity, tokens and storage are charged by usage. Your company's AI budget needs a cap, an owner and a monthly review, like any other operating expense.
  • Before migrating on price: compare total cost, not only the token price: data egress, support, permissions, and the cost of switching providers later. Launch pricing is rarely the final cost.
