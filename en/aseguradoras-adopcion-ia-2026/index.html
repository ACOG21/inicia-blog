Insurers Went From 15% to 46% AI Adoption in 2 Years: Your Industry's Playbook | inicIA

By inicIA · October 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

The 2026 Agency Universe Study — the biennial study by the Independent Insurance Agents & Brokers of America (Big "I") with Future One, surveying 1,376 agencies — delivers a striking data point: 46% of independent insurance agencies now use AI, versus 15% in 2024. It tripled in two years. The most common uses: generating marketing content (49%), analyzing coverage forms (43%), and reviewing contracts (35%). The barriers: not knowing the capabilities (60%) and security and privacy concerns (48%). If an industry as regulated and traditional as insurance moves at this speed, the question for your sector is no longer whether, but how fast.

The data in context

The study, conducted by Zeldis Research, paints an industry transforming fast: the number of independent agencies fell to about 37,000 (from 39,000 in 2024), yet three in four reported revenue increases between 2024 and 2025, and marketing budgets rose 44% (from $14,300 to $20,600). "Keeping up with AI" appeared for the first time among the industry's top challenges, cited by 43% of agencies. And there is an important nuance from another Big "I" technology council study: only 8% have AI embedded in daily workflows, and 55% have no written AI policy at all. Massive adoption, low maturity: that is the window of opportunity.

What exactly they use (the copyable playbook)

The three leading uses are a ready-to-copy playbook for almost any services business:

  1. Marketing content (49%). The entry door: newsletters, posts, descriptions. Low risk, visible return.
  2. Coverage form analysis (43%). Your business's equivalent: having AI read and summarize technical documents that consume your team's hours today.
  3. Contract reviews (35%). Automated first pass, human final review. Speed without surrendering judgment.

On the consumer side, the message is hybrid: 87% say having a human agent remains important, but 61% prefer the agent who uses AI and modern technology for faster, more personalized service. AI does not replace the relationship: it powers it.

The two barriers (and how to clear them)

The two top barriers both rose 10 points since 2024: lack of knowledge (60%) and security/privacy (48%). Clear them like this:

  • Lack of knowledge: start with one low-risk use case (content or document summarization), measure it for 30 days, then expand. The learning curve pays for itself.
  • Security and privacy: define which data may enter which tools (nothing sensitive into unvetted tools), and require your vendors' data policies in writing. Half the fear dissolves with a clear inventory.

And the study's third lesson: write your AI policy. If 55% of agencies lack one, having one puts you in the 45% operating with an edge.

Frequently asked questions

Where do these numbers come from?

The 2026 Agency Universe Study, published by Future One in partnership with Big "I", with 1,376 independent agencies surveyed by Zeldis Research. It is the most-cited biennial study of the US independent agency channel.

My industry is not insurance. Is it still useful?

The three leading uses (content, document analysis, contract review) exist in almost every services business: real estate, law firms, clinics, consultancies. The adoption pattern transfers.

Why did adoption triple so fast?

A mix of easier-to-use tools, competitive pressure, and visible results in marketing and productivity. When the neighbor shows results, the internal conversation changes.

What is the most common mistake when starting?

Starting with the most ambitious use case instead of the simplest one, and not writing a usage policy. The study shows most operate without a policy: do not be most.
