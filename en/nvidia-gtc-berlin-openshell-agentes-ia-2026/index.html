NVIDIA at GTC Berlin: OpenShell Puts AI Agents Under Hardware Watch (and the 14-Day AI Factory) | inicIA

By inicIA · October 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 21, 2026, at the GTC Berlin keynote, NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang unveiled the OpenShell Reference Architecture — a hardware-and-software recipe for containing and monitoring AI agents — and DSX POD, prebuilt modular AI factories in containers that NVIDIA says install in 14 days instead of six months (Tesla News Wire, Counterpoint Research, AI Edge Insights). The design: models run on Vera Rubin systems, agents and their tools on Vera CPUs, and BlueField-4 chips run Sentry software to watch agent behavior from outside the main system and enforce policy in hardware. "As enterprises prepare to deploy billions of AI agents, safety and control must be built into the computing architecture," Huang said. "Agents can think on Rubin, act on Vera and be governed on BlueField."

What Was Announced, Point by Point

  • Governance in silicon: the architecture extends the open-source OpenShell secure runtime. It also reaches desk-side machines like DGX Spark and DGX Station — governance doesn't stay only in the data center.
  • Who's already working with OpenShell: Anthropic, AWS, Google Cloud, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Perplexity, SAP and SpaceX Super Intelligence. Banks BBVA and Santander are reviewing the specification.
  • The fine print (via The Register): it requires NVIDIA hardware, OpenShell still has open security issues on GitHub, and it collects telemetry by default unless the user opts out. Governance yes, but with vendor dependence.
  • DSX POD and greener factories: PODs are validated at the factory before shipping; closed-loop liquid cooling that, per NVIDIA, cuts cooling water use by up to 100%; DSX MaxLPS software produces the same AI output with up to 30% less power. CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Lambda, Nebius and Nscale plan to use it.
  • The Europe message: NVIDIA has invested nearly $10 billion in Europe over 12 months and works with 12,000+ partners; Huang's message is that Europe should produce AI, not just consume it (Counterpoint). The prior week, Germany's economy ministry signed an industrial partnership MoU with NVIDIA (Reuters).
  • The curious detail: Bloomberg's live coverage reported protesters briefly interrupted the keynote.

What It Means for Your Business (Even If You Don't Buy a POD)

  1. Agent governance is becoming infrastructure: it goes beyond "careful prompts": it's compute architecture. When evaluating agent vendors, ask about their containment, monitoring and permission controls, not just their capabilities.
  2. Vendor dependence hardens too: NVIDIA's solution requires its hardware. Before locking in, assess whether your agent strategy depends on a single stack — the Tencent/QClaw lesson still applies.
  3. Deployment speed = competitive advantage: 14 days vs. 6 months to bring an AI factory online. At enterprise scale, the bottleneck has moved from the model to infrastructure. Plan your adoption with that reality.
  4. Data sovereignty, the practical version: the Europe message applies to your company — running open models on your own infrastructure is a real option for data you don't want to send to anyone.
