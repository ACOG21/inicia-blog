Meta seeks up to $95B in capital for its AI: $35B now and $60B more after its new Class C shares

By inicIA · October 27, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 26, 2026, Meta announced plans to raise up to $95 billion in its largest-ever equity fundraising, to pay for its AI infrastructure expansion. The company launched public offerings of about $35 billion in common and preferred stock, and plans a separate at-the-market sale program of up to $60 billion, to start after its new non-voting Class C shares begin trading, expected in the first quarter of 2027. The company said it has not yet finalized the timing or terms of that second part.

The details that matter

  • Chip suppliers want in: Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom showed interest of up to $5 billion in total in Class A shares, according to Reuters.
  • The market reaction: Meta shares fell about 1.4% in morning trading on the announcement.
  • The sector context: earlier in 2026, Alphabet had already increased its equity offerings to $84.75 billion. Big tech moved from funding itself with cash to tapping debt and equity to pay for data centers and chips.
  • The same day, another giant deal: SpaceX said an Apollo Global Management affiliate will buy AI chips and server hardware and lease them to it, in a deal with total principal payments of about $40 billion, per the same Reuters coverage.

The question analysts are asking

  • Circular capital: when chip suppliers finance one of their biggest customers, the money goes around in a circle. William Blair analysts wrote that they expect investors to keep questioning the financing and the potential for future capital raises.
  • The two-part reading: the same William Blair note says the financing reinforces both Meta's confidence in the long-term returns from AI investment and how capital-intensive pursuing that opportunity has become.
  • What to watch as an AI buyer: your provider's price depends on this capital race. A provider funding infrastructure with new capital can hold prices low today and change terms tomorrow. Ask for contracts with clear prices and limits, not just demos.

The honest note

  • Sources: Reuters (October 26, 2026), with the William Blair note cited in that coverage.
  • What is not closed yet: the timing and terms of the up-to-$60 billion program, which depend on Class C shares starting to trade.

Frequently asked questions

Has Meta already raised $95 billion?

No: it launched offerings of about $35 billion and plans a program of up to $60 billion more, starting after Class C shares begin trading in the first quarter of 2027.

Why does Meta need so much capital?

To pay for data centers, chips and cloud infrastructure to compete with OpenAI and Anthropic, according to Reuters.

What are Class C shares?

Meta's new non-voting shares, expected to begin trading in the first quarter of 2027.

Does this affect the price of the AI I buy?

Indirectly: the big providers' capital race supports prices and capacity today, and can change terms tomorrow. That is why prices and limits belong in the contract.
