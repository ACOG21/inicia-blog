OpenAI billed more than $10 billion in a quarter for the first time

By inicIA · November 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

OpenAI's revenue topped $10 billion in the third quarter of 2026, for the first time in a single quarter, according to Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal. The figure is almost 50% higher than the $6.7 billion of the second quarter and almost triple the same quarter a year earlier. The figures were shared with investors on a call on November 10, led by CFO Sarah Friar and President Greg Brockman. OpenAI, as a private company, did not publish the figures: they are figures reported by media with access to the call and to investor documents, not audited financial statements.

The quarter in numbers

  • The jump is almost 50% in one quarter: OpenAI billed about $5.7 billion in the first quarter and $6.7 billion in the second, growth of 18% between the two. The jump to more than $10 billion in the third is growth of almost 50% in three months, driven by tighter pricing, discounts, better models and a sharper focus on business products.
  • The loss grew with the revenue: the adjusted operating loss reached $10.9 billion in the third quarter, against $10.1 billion in the second. The adjusted operating margin improved from -150% to -109%: the company loses less per dollar of revenue, but the loss in dollars rose.
  • The annualized pace is around $50 billion: according to Bloomberg, the annualized revenue run rate reached about $50 billion at the end of September. Brockman said at a Wall Street Journal event on November 4 that annualized revenue grew about 70% during the third quarter and that the enterprise business roughly doubled.
  • Anthropic billed more in the same quarter: Anthropic reported about $17 billion of revenue in the third quarter, with an operating loss of $567 million, according to its SEC filing cited by the Wall Street Journal, and an annualized run rate of $81 billion. The comparison has a nuance: Anthropic includes sales through cloud partners in its figures, while OpenAI reports only its share of some partner sales.

What this says about your AI provider

  • Growth validates demand, and the loss validates cost: more than $10 billion in one quarter confirms that companies pay for AI at scale. The $10.9 billion loss in the same quarter confirms that serving that demand costs more than it brings in. Both figures are true at once, and both affect future pricing.
  • Price pressure points in two directions: growth of almost 50% in one quarter gives OpenAI room to hold prices; the $10.9 billion loss gives OpenAI pressure to raise them. The enterprise business, which doubled, is the segment where the company charges most and where increases are felt first.
  • Provider continuity is a business risk: your operation depends on a private provider that does not publish audited financial statements. The figures arrive through media reports on investor calls. The question for your company is what happens to your agents if your provider changes prices, changes terms or changes hands.
  • The IPO stays on the calendar: OpenAI filed confidentially for its IPO in June 2026, and Anthropic has already filed its public S-1. The IPO will force the publication of audited figures, and on that day reading the numbers will stop depending on media reports.

What to do in your business this week

  • Review your concentration on one provider: write down which part of your operation depends on OpenAI: models, agents, integrations and monthly costs. A second provider with an alternative model is insurance, not a luxury.
  • Read the contract, not the headline: the headline says $10 billion of revenue; your invoice says the price per token of your plan. Review the price-change and terms-change clauses of your agreement with the provider.
  • Separate the provider's revenue from your return: OpenAI billing more does not mean your agent earns more. Your metric is cost per finished task and revenue per served customer, measured in your business.
  • Mark the IPO on your calendar: when OpenAI publishes audited figures, reading its financial health will be more solid. Until then, treat every reported figure as what it is: a reported figure, not an audited one.

Note: this article summarizes coverage by Bloomberg, the Wall Street Journal, The Information, TechCrunch and CNBC, with figures shared with investors on November 10, 2026. OpenAI did not publicly confirm the figures. Anthropic's figures are from its SEC filing. None of OpenAI's figures is an audited financial statement.
