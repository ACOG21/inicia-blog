OpenAI Cuts Its Valuation to $1.25 Trillion and Anthropic Shows an $80.7B Loss: How to Read the IPO Numbers

By inicIA · October 25, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

Two numbers dominated the AI economics conversation on October 23, 2026. First, OpenAI cut its target valuation from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion in the roughly $30 billion round it is negotiating, according to Bloomberg. Second, Anthropic's draft IPO prospectus shows $16.3 billion in revenue for the first half of 2026 and a net loss of $80.7 billion, of which about $72 billion comes from non-cash accounting adjustments tied to convertible instruments. Neither figure means what it looks like at first glance, and both matter for your business because they set the future price of the tools you use.

OpenAI: a 10.7% cut that is still a 47% rise

  • The cut: moving from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion is a $150 billion reduction, about 10.7%, in the price being discussed in a private round still under negotiation.
  • The context: the March 2026 valuation after a reported $122 billion round was $852 billion. Even at $1.25 trillion, the new target sits about 47% above that mark.
  • Why now: tech stocks faced selling pressure after mixed results from companies like Meta and Tesla, and investors are looking harder at whether commercial revenue grows as fast as spending on chips, data centers and electricity.
  • What did not change: the round under negotiation is still about $30 billion, and OpenAI had already reported revenue on a $50 billion to $70 billion trajectory depending on accounting treatment.

Anthropic: the $80.7 billion loss, taken apart

  • Real revenue growth: $16.3 billion in the first half of 2026, with the third quarter already reported at $16.8 billion at its October 14 investor day. The company is growing fast.
  • The loss is mostly paper: about $72 billion of the net loss comes from non-cash accounting adjustments for convertible financial instruments, which get revalued as the company's valuation rises. Paradoxically, part of that "loss" appears because the company is worth more.
  • Operating spending is real: Anthropic racked up more than $12 billion in operating expenses last year, according to coverage of the UK package that cited the figure. The real question is whether revenue catches up with infrastructure before capital gets more expensive.
  • The calendar: Anthropic targets a mid-November IPO at a reported valuation between $1.8 trillion and $2 trillion. That prospectus is the first serious public look at its books, and the market will read it line by line.

What this means for your business

  1. AI prices will keep moving: when labs negotiate valuations down and publish giant accounting losses, pressure to monetize rises. Expect price, limit and packaging changes, and contract with exit clauses.
  2. Diversify providers before you are forced to: the price war among OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta is your best ally while it lasts. A business tied to one provider pays whatever price that provider sets when the market cools.
  3. Measure cost per outcome, not per token: the metric that protects your margin is what each completed task costs (a ticket resolved, an appointment booked, a document classified), not the model's list price.
  4. Capital is still available, at a more demanding price: a 10% correction in a private negotiation prices risk after months of euphoria.

The honest note

  • All of this is reported, not confirmed by the companies: OpenAI's valuation comes from Bloomberg and Anthropic's prospectus is a draft. Negotiations are ongoing and figures can change before closing.
  • Anthropic's loss is not cash burn: do not use it as proof that the company "lost $80.7 billion in cash". The distinction between accounting loss and cash burn is the central point of this article.
  • Sources: Bloomberg (October 23, 2026, via coverage of the valuation adjustment), Anthropic's draft IPO prospectus and Anthropic's October 14, 2026 investor day.

Frequently asked questions

Did OpenAI lose $150 billion?

No. It lowered its target valuation in private negotiations from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion. That is an adjustment to the price under discussion, not a realized loss.

Did Anthropic lose $80.7 billion in cash?

No. About $72 billion of that net loss is non-cash accounting adjustments for convertible instruments. Its first-half 2026 revenue was $16.3 billion.

Why does this matter if I only use AI tools?

Because monetization pressure sets future prices, limits and packages. A provider that needs to show profitability changes its terms faster than one that only needs to grow.

Is the AI boom over?

There is no evidence of that in these figures. There is a price correction in private negotiations and greater scrutiny of profitability, which is different from a collapse in demand.
