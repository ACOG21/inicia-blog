Le Chonk: the 1-trillion-parameter European open model now has its weights out

By inicIA · November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

Le Chonk is the nickname of Mistral Large 4, Mistral AI's 1-trillion-parameter model. On October 29, 2026 Mistral published its full weights on Hugging Face: any business can download the model, fine-tune it and run it on its own infrastructure. Only 49 billion parameters activate per token, thanks to its mixture-of-experts architecture. The European open route is now verifiable in hand, with public figures and a public license.

What shipped on October 29

  • The full weights, downloadable: the Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B model arrived on Hugging Face three weeks after its API-only preview opened on October 6. The download lets you run it, fine-tune it and build products on it, with the usual vLLM and SGLang tooling and an NVIDIA NIM container.
  • 1 trillion parameters, 49 billion active: Large 4 is a mixture of experts with 128 routed experts, of which 4 plus 1 shared expert process each token. At 52 billion counting embedding and output layers, the model delivers frontier-class capacity at a much lower per-token compute cost than its total size.
  • Multimodal with 1-million context: the model reads text and images, handles more than 160 languages, including every official language of the European Union, and works with a 1-million-token context window. Mistral trained it from scratch on its own infrastructure in Europe.
  • An open license with one clear limit: Large 4 ships under the Mistral Ecosystem Open-Weight License, a modified MIT-style license. Commercial use is allowed, and selling the model itself as a hosted service requires a separate commercial agreement with Mistral.

What the numbers say (and whose they are)

  • 48% on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index: tied with GLM-5.3 and using 47% fewer tokens per task, according to the independent measurement Mistral itself cites. This is the strongest figure of the launch, because it comes from an outside evaluator.
  • 41% on Terminal-Bench 4.0: up from 28% in the preview, with 30% fewer output tokens. The improvement between the preview and the final weights came with three more weeks of reinforcement learning.
  • The rest are Mistral's claims: the company says it is the best open model on Cognition's FrontierCode coding test and that it refuses malicious cyber requests well. Those figures are the vendor's until independent evaluations land at scale.
  • API pricing for comparison: through the hosted API, Large 4 costs $0.70 per million input tokens, $0.07 for cached input and $2.10 per million output tokens. Open weights remove the vendor, and the bill arrives in hardware and operations.

What to do in your business this week

  • Price the real cost of self-hosting: the full weights run to about 1 terabyte in FP8 and around 0.5 terabytes in NVFP4, before the cache that grows with every long conversation. Get a GPU and operations quote before celebrating the zero download price.
  • Use the API to test before installing: Large 4 is also available through the API in Mistral Studio, OpenRouter, NVIDIA-hosted endpoints and Microsoft Foundry. Run your real tasks through the API and measure quality and cost per finished task.
  • Check the license against your business model: if your plan is to sell the model itself as a hosted service, you need Mistral's commercial agreement. If you use it inside your product, the open license covers you.
  • Compare with the closed option you already use: the open model gives you auditability and data sovereignty. The closed model gives you operations without infrastructure. The decision is between control and convenience, with numbers on the table.
