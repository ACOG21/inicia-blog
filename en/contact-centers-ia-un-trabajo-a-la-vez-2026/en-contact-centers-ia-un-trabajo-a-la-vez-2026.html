Contact centers buy AI one job at a time: voice, resets and analytics first

By inicIA · November 2, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

Contact center customers are buying AI one narrow job at a time. Per CMSWire's analysis of earnings reports from eight vendors as of October 28, 2026, the most common first AI jobs are answering missed calls, resetting passwords and analyzing call recordings. The pattern repeats vendor after vendor: the customer picks a high-volume job, proves the return and then buys more. Adoption is broad by customer count and shallow by share of conversations.

The numbers, with their definitions

  • Five9, Q2 2026: AI revenue was 15% of subscription revenue, up from 9% a year earlier, at $39 million in the quarter, up 78%.
  • NiCE, Q2 2026: AI annual recurring revenue reached $362 million, 15% of cloud revenue, up 52%, including the Cognigy business it acquired.
  • Genesys, Q2 FY2027: Genesys Cloud AI annual recurring revenue topped $400 million, growing more than twice as fast as total Genesys Cloud ARR, near $2.9 billion. By CMSWire's math, AI is roughly 14% of that total.
  • RingCentral, Q3 2026: customers using at least one paid AI product account for about 15% of total annual recurring revenue, more than $400 million, doubling year over year. That figure counts those customers' full spend, not AI revenue alone.
  • The caveat that matters: each company measures something different, and none breaks out how much AI contributes to profit. Comparing the percentages without reading the definition compares different things.

What to copy from that pattern

  • Pick a job with volume and a measurable outcome: missed calls, resets and call summaries have daily volume and a verifiable result. A multi-step autonomous agent is the next project, not the first.
  • Define the return before signing: response time, calls answered, resets resolved without a human agent and cost per contact. Without that baseline, any success report is opinion.
  • Depth comes later: RingCentral's CEO noted that AI still resolves a small share of calls on its platform, even as the number of customers buying AI doubles. Breadth of purchase and depth of use are two different metrics.
  • Voice is the entry point: Twilio reported that self-serve voice, the main entry point for AI-native customers, grew more than 50% year over year. The phone remains the channel where the customer feels the difference first.

Note: this article summarizes CMSWire's October 28, 2026 analysis of earnings reports from Five9, NiCE, Genesys, RingCentral, Twilio, Sprinklr and 8x8, plus NiCE's announcement on the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant. Figures are company-reported and definitions differ between companies, as explained in the text.

Frequently asked questions

Which AI job do contact centers buy first?

Narrow, high-volume jobs: missed calls, password resets and call analytics, per earnings reports from eight vendors analyzed by CMSWire.

What share of revenue does AI represent at those vendors?

Roughly 12% to 15% on the comparable measurements each company discloses, under different definitions: Five9 counts AI product revenue, NiCE counts AI recurring revenue and RingCentral counts the full spend of customers with at least one AI product.

Is contact center AI adoption deep?

It is broad and shallow: many customers buy AI, and the share of conversations AI resolves alone remains small on the platforms reported.

Where should a small business start?

With one narrow job with daily volume and a measurable outcome, with a cost and time baseline before signing, and with expansion conditioned on demonstrated return.
