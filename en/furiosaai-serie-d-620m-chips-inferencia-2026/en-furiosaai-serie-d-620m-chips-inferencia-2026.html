FuriosaAI raised $620M at $2.9B: the AI inference chip race has another player with real production

By inicIA · November 3, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

South Korea's FuriosaAI announced on October 28 and 29, 2026 the close of its $620 million Series D (KRW 820 billion), at a post-money valuation of about $2.9 billion (KRW 3.8 trillion) and a pre-money valuation of $2.28 billion, per the company release and HPCwire, TokenPost and Bloomberg coverage. The largest investor was Korea's National Growth Fund, with $280 million; international investors contributed $110 million, with Infinitum the largest of that group. The round quadruples the prior $735 million valuation reported by Bloomberg.

What FuriosaAI does and where the money goes

  • Inference, not training: FuriosaAI designs processors optimized for inference, the stage where a trained model generates answers. That is where the recurring cost of every business running agents lives.
  • RNGD in mass production since January 2026: its second-generation accelerator ships as a 180-watt air-cooled card and in server configurations with multiple accelerators.
  • Production targets: the company targets 20,000 RNGD accelerators in 2026 and more than 40,000 in 2027, with memory and factory capacity secured, per the company.
  • Named customers: Samsung SDS, LG Group and Upstage appear among RNGD customers and partners, with a Samsung SDS NPU-as-a-service and Upstage's Daum search summary cited as deployments.
  • Third generation with Broadcom: the capital funds more RNGD production, a stronger software ecosystem and a third accelerator in development with Broadcom, a 2-nanometer multi-die design with HBM4 memory.

What it means for your business even if you never buy a chip

  • Cost per answer falls when supply grows: every new inference provider pressures the price you pay per million tokens and per agent in production. Hardware diversification is one force behind the model price war.
  • Energy efficiency is direct cost: a 180-watt air-cooled card changes the economics of serving models in owned data centers and in the cloud. Ask your provider what 1,000 answers cost, not just the model price.
  • Public capital is in the game too: nearly half the round comes from state-directed Korean funds, per Science Monitor's analysis, which also notes the absence of direct investment from chip giants on the shareholder list. The valuation is funded ambition, not verified sales.
  • Verify deployment before betting: mass production and named customers are stronger signals than a round. The company, founded in 2017, reaches this point with product in customers' hands, not just a prototype.

Note: this article summarizes FuriosaAI's October 28 and 29, 2026 release and HPCwire, TokenPost, TechStartups and Bloomberg via Apex News coverage. Production figures are company targets and the valuation is post-round, not an independent measurement of sales.
