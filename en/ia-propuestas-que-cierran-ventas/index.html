Direct answer

Proposals that close with AI are built around the client's specific problem: a brief diagnosis in their words, a solution in timed steps, proof with verifiable data, and a single easy next step. AI generates the document in minutes from your call notes and your real cases, using your winning structure as a template. You review the commercial angle and the recommendation. Selling at higher prices comes from clarity and proof: the client understands what they buy, what they receive, and what happens next.

The structure the client actually reads

Clients read proposals looking for three answers: did they understand my problem?, what exactly will they do?, what do I do now? The structure that works:

  1. Your situation (in their words). Two or three lines that repeat the problem as the client described it on the call. When they recognize themselves, they keep reading.
  2. What we will do and in how long. Numbered steps, with deliverables and relative dates (week 1, week 2). No technical smoke: results and activities.
  3. Brief proof. A data point, a case with permission, or an explained process. Proof reduces perceived risk; perceived risk lowers the price they accept.
  4. Investment and scope. What is included, what is out, and how payment works. Clarity here prevents negotiation by confusion.
  5. Single next step. One action: a 15-minute call, a signature, a link. Two options close more than five.

What AI does in this flow (and what you do)

AI takes your notes or the call transcript and: extracts the problem in the client's phrases, orders the solution with your template, inserts your saved proof (cases, data, process), and generates the ready-to-review document. It also creates the short version for WhatsApp or email and the case FAQ page.

You provide: the recommendation (which option suits this client), the price, and any scope adjustment. AI speeds up the paper; your judgment closes the sale. No proposal leaves without your final read.

Selling at higher prices: where the accepted price comes from

The price they accept rises when three things are visible: the cost of the problem (what the client loses each month without solving it), the closeness of the proof (similar cases, clear process), and the ease of the next step. AI helps you quantify the cost of the problem with the data the client gave on the call (prospect volume, response rate, team hours). That figure, in the proposal, puts your investment in proportion.

What lowers perceived price: long tool lists, vague promises, and documents that talk about you instead of the client. The winning proposal talks about the client on page one and about you only as the means to their result.

The error that kills good proposals

Sending the proposal and waiting. The proposal closes in the conversation around it: notice before sending ("I'll send it today at 4, we review tomorrow in 15 minutes"), delivery with a booked call, and follow-up at 24 and 72 hours with a contribution (an answer, a data point). AI schedules and executes that follow-up; you join the closing call with the document already read.

Your template ready in an afternoon

  2. Save your proof in a folder: cases with permission, process data, FAQs.
  3. Define your minimum call notes: problem, volume, estimated cost, decision, and date.
  4. Generate the next proposal from notes and review total time: goal, under 30 minutes of your time.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI set prices for me?

AI organizes your pricing structure and calculates scenarios with your rules. The final price and scope decision is yours: it is your commercial judgment and your responsibility to the client.

How long does a proposal take with this system?

With clear notes and a ready template, the draft comes out in minutes. Your commercial review takes 10 to 20 minutes. Total time drops from hours to under half an hour per proposal.

Does it work for personalized services?

Yes: the template fixes the structure and AI adapts problem, steps, and proof to each client from the notes. The personalization lives in the content; the repetition, in the form.

What if the client asks for a discount?

Return to the cost of the problem and to scope: adjust scope before price. A clear proposal makes that conversation simpler because each part of the price has a visible deliverable.
