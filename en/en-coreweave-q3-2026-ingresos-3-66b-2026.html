CoreWeave billed $3.66B in a quarter and owes $46.9B: the AI cloud grows and borrows at the same time

By inicIA · November 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 10, 2026, CoreWeave reported $3.66 billion in revenue for the third quarter of 2026, up 168% from $1.365 billion in the same quarter of 2025, above the $3.55 billion analysts expected. Its order backlog reached $132.5 billion, a record, up 138% from a year ago. In the same quarter, the company posted a $709 million net loss, versus $110 million a year ago, with $846 million in net interest and $14.5 billion in capital spending. The company raised its 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0–$13.3 billion and expects adjusted profitability in the first half of 2028. The stock rose 24.71% on November 11, its best day since April 2025, according to CNBC.

The numbers of the quarter

  • Revenue: $3.66 billion, up 168% year over year. Growth comes from demand for Nvidia-chip compute capacity that CoreWeave rents to AI labs and enterprises.
  • Order backlog: $132.5 billion, a record. Up from $104 billion in June. It is the value of signed contracts still to be delivered: the company must bring facilities online, secure power and obtain equipment before recognizing that revenue.
  • Net loss: $709 million. Net interest expense of $846 million exceeded operating income of $207 million. Adjusted operating income was $379 million and adjusted EBITDA was $2.26 billion, a 62% margin.
  • Capital spending: $14.5 billion in the quarter. Full-year capital spending guidance rose to $37–$39 billion. Total debt is around $46.9 billion, and recourse debt rose to $41.6 billion from $31.4 billion in June.
  • Customer prepayments: about $9 billion expected in the next three months. These are deposits and prepayments customers make to secure future capacity. More than $3 billion relates to re-contracting infrastructure that is already paid off, according to the earnings call.

Why the market celebrated a quarter with losses

  • Demand is signed: a $132.5 billion backlog is 36 times the quarter's revenue. Customers are signing multi-year contracts and paying in advance to secure capacity.
  • Operating margins are improving: adjusted EBITDA of 62% and adjusted operating income of $379 million show the core business generates money. The loss comes from interest; the core business generates money.
  • Guidance went up: the company raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $13.0–$13.3 billion and expects annualized run-rate revenue of $20–$21 billion by year-end. The business points upward, with a high financing cost.
  • The risk sits in financing: fourth-quarter interest is estimated at $1.05–$1.15 billion, close to the full year's adjusted operating income. If the cost of money rises or customers pay late, the model tightens. There is also a securities class action in New Jersey alleging the company overstated its ability to meet demand.

What to do with this news if you buy AI

  • Compute capacity has a queue: if your vendor depends on CoreWeave or similar clouds, ask how it secures capacity for your workloads. A record backlog means new capacity already has an owner.
  • Read the backlog for what it is: the $132.5 billion is signed contracts with delivery conditions. A vendor that talks about its backlog as if it were cash in hand is selling you the headline. Ask for the balance sheet.
  • The cost of money is the cost of your AI: the interest CoreWeave pays ends up in the price you pay per token. When financing gets more expensive, AI prices move.
  • Diversify vendor risk: the AI cloud has several sources of compute and several companies. The vendor with more than one source of compute is the one that keeps operating when one source tightens.

Note: this article summarizes CoreWeave's third-quarter 2026 results, published November 10, 2026, and coverage in Reuters, The Motley Fool and TechTimes. Revenue, backlog, loss and interest figures are from the earnings release. The order backlog is the value of signed contracts to be delivered, pending recognition as revenue.
