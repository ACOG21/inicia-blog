The Business of Chatbots That 'Fall in Love': 45 Million Users and Almost No Revenue | inicIA

By inicIA · October 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

Character.AI —founded in 2021 by ex-Google researchers Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, with public beta in September 2022— reached 45 million active users in September 2025 and generated about $50 million in revenue in 2025, 66% more than the $30 million of 2024. In September 2024, Google paid $2.7 billion for a non-exclusive license to its technology and brought the co-founders back to Google DeepMind. Replika, the category pioneer since 2017, has accumulated about 25 million users in more than 150 countries and 20+ languages. And yet the business is a paradox: with 45 million users and $50M in revenue, Character.AI makes around $1.10 per active user per year —only about 8% of free users convert to the $9.99/month premium plan. Attention does not convert into money automatically.

The category in numbers

  • Character.AI: 45M active users (September 2025); ~$50M revenue in 2025; c.ai+ subscription at $9.99/month; $1B valuation in 2023; $2.7B Google deal in September 2024.
  • Replika: ~25M total users, 150+ countries, 20+ languages; revenue estimates vary by source (the reported range runs from ~$5M to ~$30M annually) —a sign of a business shrinking while the category grows.
  • The paradox: 45M users spending on average up to 2 hours a day on the platform generate $50M a year. One category analysis estimates real consumer companion-app spend in 2025 at ~$120M —far from inflated projections in the tens of billions that mix enterprise productivity assistants with emotional companions.

The risk: emotional AI is the category with the highest legal liability

In January 2026, TechCrunch reported that Google and Character.AI were negotiating the first major settlements in lawsuits over teen deaths linked to companion chatbots —including the case of 14-year-old Sewell Setzer III. Character.AI responded by restricting under-18s: a 2-hour daily conversation cap starting October 2025 and a full ban on open-ended chat for minors no later than November 25, 2025, with age verification. The lesson is brutal and clear: the more intimate the emotional bond your AI creates, the greater your legal liability.

3 business lessons

  1. Engagement does not equal revenue. Millions of hours of use do not guarantee monetization. Design the business model before optimizing product addiction —or you will end up with 45M users and $1.10 per head.
  2. The greater the emotional intimacy, the greater the legal risk. A companion chatbot needs safeguards a sales chatbot will never need. The cost of neglect here is not an annoyed customer: it is lawsuits and regulation.
  3. The strategic value may be in the technology, not the product. Google paid $2.7B for the license and the talent, not for the 45M free users. Sometimes the real business is what your AI knows how to do, not whom you sell it to.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the AI companion chatbot market?

It depends on what you count. Inflated projections mix in enterprise productivity assistants; real consumer companion-app spend in 2025 is estimated at ~$120M. Users number in the tens of millions; money, much less.

Why don't people pay for these chatbots?

Because conversation feels free once the marginal cost is zero. Only ~8% of Character.AI's free users convert to the $9.99/month plan. Attention does not convert on its own: you have to design the value worth paying for.

What happened with the teen cases?

In January 2026 it was reported that Google and Character.AI were negotiating the first settlements in lawsuits over teen deaths linked to companion chatbots. The company restricted minor access and banned open-ended chat for under-18s.

What is Replika?

The pioneer of AI companions, launched in 2017: a chatbot designed as a virtual friend or partner, with ~25M users in 150+ countries. Its model is subscription-based, with higher revenue per user but smaller reach than Character.AI's freemium.
