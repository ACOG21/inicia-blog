OpenAI Astra for Law: AI Is Going Vertical by Industry (Yours Is Next) | inicIA

By inicIA · October 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

On September 17, 2026, OpenAI launched Astra for Law: GPT-6 Astra configured for professional legal work, with a search index of more than 230 million URLs of US case law, statutes, regulations, and administrative decisions, instructions tuned for legal analysis, and firm-grade privacy controls. The case-law corpus comes from the Free Law Project (CourtListener) and covers over 99.9% of published precedential case law. Firms like Sullivan & Cromwell, Ropes & Gray, and Cooley co-developed tools on the platform; Harvey and Legora build products on top. The lesson for your business: generic AI is no longer enough. Specialized AI wins: your model + your data + your rules. Your industry is next.

What it is exactly (and what it is not)

Important: Astra for Law is NOT a new model. It is the same GPT-6 Astra OpenAI already sold, combined with three things: its own legal index updated daily, instructions shaping how it researches and cites, and firm-grade controls (trusted access, zero data retention). It appears in ChatGPT's model picker as "GPT-6 Astra Law" and in the API as gpt-6-astra-law.

The launch also included 26 partner plugins (Thomson Reuters HighQ, Relativity, Clio, Intapp, iManage, DeepJudge) connecting ChatGPT to the software firms already pay for. What changes is less the quality of any single answer than the place where the lawyer starts asking the question.

The numbers that matter (with their limits)

On the published benchmark —200 questions from Vals AI's private validation set, at maximum reasoning effort— Astra for Law passed the correctness check on 54.0% of items, versus 38.7% for GPT-6 Astra with web search. A 40% relative improvement. But be honest about the limits: 200 questions is a small sample, from a third party, and not a general measure of "legal accuracy." OpenAI itself designs the product on the premise that a lawyer verifies every cited authority.

The structural number is the index: 230 million URLs with sources added daily. In applied business AI, whoever holds the best curated corpus of their domain beats whoever holds the best generic model.

The repeating pattern: verticalization

This is already the third nearly identical launch from a major lab into the legal vertical in five months. The pattern is clear and will repeat in your industry: first the general model, then the specialized configuration with the sector's corpus, the sector's partners, and the sector's controls.

For your business, the question is no longer "do I use ChatGPT or not?" but "who builds me the AI version that knows MY business: my documents, my processes, my rules?" A generic chatbot answers; a specialized agent with your data executes.

What it means for your industry

If you are a lawyer, realtor, insurer, clinician, or educator: the bar rose. Your competitors will not use "AI" in the abstract; they will use AI trained on their operation's knowledge. The advantage is not in the model —everyone will have access to powerful models— but in the configuration: your data, your instructions, your integrations, your supervision.

Frequently asked questions

Does Astra for Law replace lawyers?

No: it amplifies their research and drafting work. The design assumes a professional verifies every authority. Firms use it to analyze, draft, and redline under their own standards.

Does it work outside the United States?

The research index covers US law. Professionals elsewhere can learn from the pattern (model + own corpus + controls), but local rules require separate tools and verification.

How much does it cost?

Access starts as Trusted Access for selected firms in ChatGPT and Codex. OpenAI published no pricing; it is an enterprise product with controlled access, not a public subscription.

What should non-law businesses copy?

The formula: powerful model + curated corpus of your domain + your operation's instructions + integrations with your software + human supervision. That is the recipe for AI that actually returns value.
