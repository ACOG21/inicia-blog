OpenAI Seeks $30B at $1.4T+ Valuation Ahead of Anthropic IPO: What It Means for Your Business | inicIA

By inicIA · October 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 20, 2026, Reuters reported that OpenAI is raising approximately $30 billion in a round that would value it at more than $1.4 trillion, aiming to close before Anthropic's IPO expected in mid-November. SoftBank Group and Abu Dhabi's MGX are reportedly in talks to participate, alongside other existing investors. It would be a jump of more than 64% from the $852 billion valuation of March 2026. OpenAI argues its growth accelerated after the launch of GPT-6 Astra: annualized revenue reportedly reached ~$50 billion by end of September, projected at $70 billion by year-end. On the other side, Anthropic reported $16.8 billion in third-quarter revenue (+45% quarter over quarter), and some investors are pushing for its IPO to price closer to $1.5 trillion than the $2 trillion the company wanted. None of this is closed: these are reported talks, not a signed round.

Why two labs are fighting over the same money

  • The capital pool is narrow: few institutions have deep enough pockets for trillion-dollar valuations; OpenAI and Anthropic are knocking on the same doors (sovereign wealth funds, long-only funds). Whoever sets their price first avoids letting the other set the comparable.
  • Infrastructure devours cash: the Financial Times reported OpenAI could burn nearly $280 billion toward 2030 on compute and data centers. Private capital lets it set terms without the quarterly scrutiny of a public company.
  • Anthropic's IPO will be the thermometer: a successful (or weak) public listing will set the market multiple for the entire foundation-model industry — and OpenAI prefers its price to be set first.

What this tells an AI buyer (beyond the headlines)

  • Competition benefits you: two giants fighting for capital and corporate customers translates into better prices and better terms for buyers. This year's model price war has its roots here.
  • Look at continuity, not glamour: a vendor burning billions needs its business to work. When evaluating an AI provider for your company, ask about runway, infrastructure contracts, and what happens to your data if the vendor changes hands or strategy.
  • Diversify your bets: if your operation depends on a single model or vendor, one round, one IPO, or one price change leaves you exposed. Multi-model architecture is no longer a luxury: it is risk management.

How to evaluate your vendor in this context

  1. Ask for 24-month pricing: with valuations at highs, API prices can move. Ask for pricing schemes with a horizon, not just today's rate.
  2. Demand an exit: your prompts, data, and workflows should migrate to another model without rewriting everything. Lock-in is the most expensive tax in AI.
  3. Measure total cost, not the token: infrastructure, monitoring, guardrails, and remediation (like the Bank of America case we cover today) weigh more than the price per million tokens.

Frequently asked questions

Has OpenAI already closed the $30 billion round?

No. As of Oct 20, 2026 it is a round in talks, reported by Reuters based on people familiar with the matter. OpenAI and SoftBank declined to comment; Anthropic and MGX did not respond. Terms may change.

What would OpenAI be worth with this round?

More than $1.4 trillion, versus $852 billion in its March 2026 round. It would be one of the highest private valuations in history.

Why does it want to close before Anthropic's IPO?

Because both compete for the same pool of institutional investors, and a public Anthropic IPO would set a market comparable OpenAI would rather avoid. "OpenAI could want to set its own valuation and not have to worry about a comp," Edward Best of Willkie Farr & Gallagher told Reuters.

How does this affect me if my business uses AI?

Competition between labs translates into lower prices and better products for you — but also vendor volatility. The smart play is diversifying models, demanding data portability, and negotiating pricing with a horizon.
