The investigation that brought down Nvidia's CEO: what is known and what remains unproven

By inicIA · November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and director of Nvidia on November 11, 2026, effective immediately, after 33 years leading the company. Three days earlier, on November 8, Huang admitted to his staff that he had been unfaithful and apologized. The board opened an independent investigation into reports of a foreign intelligence operation and into the customer Megaspeed. On November 17, the board said it does not intend to reinstate Huang as CEO. What is admitted and documented is clear; the espionage and chip-diversion allegations remain unproven.

The verified timeline

  • October 29: The New York Times reported that US officials regard a Chinese executive at a Singapore-based Nvidia customer as an intelligence asset, and that surveillance showed a close relationship between her and Huang.
  • November 5: the chairs of three House committees asked Huang for a closed-door briefing. The briefing is now set for December 1 and 2, with Huang and general counsel Tim Teter answering questions.
  • November 8: Huang told his staff he had been unfaithful to his wife and apologized to his family and those involved. The board announced an independent investigation by independent directors with outside legal counsel.
  • November 11: the board accepted Huang's resignation as president, CEO and director, effective immediately, according to the 8-K filing accepted by the SEC on November 12 at 8:52 a.m. Eastern time.
  • November 12: CFO Colette Kress, 59, took over as interim CEO, and Harvey C. Jones, 73, became the first board chair since the company's founding.
  • November 16: the Commerce Department announced new rules to prevent remote access to advanced AI chips and servers, closing a route that let companies use Nvidia chips from data centers abroad. The same day, Commerce and Justice announced that the Justice Department is conducting a criminal investigation of Megaspeed.
  • November 17: the board said it does not intend to reinstate Huang as CEO, amid a push by the former CEO's allies for his return. The board also formed a committee to oversee national security policies, export-control compliance and global trade risk management.

What is known and what remains unproven

  • Admitted by Huang: the affair, in a message to his staff on November 8, with an apology included. Huang said: I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board's independent investigation.
  • Documented by the board: the resignation accepted on November 11, the independent investigation in progress, the interim CEO and the new board chair, and the November 17 decision not to reinstate him.
  • Alleged, unproven: that Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive, is an asset of China's Ministry of State Security. The assessment rests on anonymous officials and an unpublished memo. China calls it speculation without foundation, and parts of the intelligence community doubt it, according to the reporting.
  • Under investigation, with no violation established: the Commerce Department has investigated since September 2024 whether Megaspeed diverted Nvidia chips to China. Megaspeed's Malaysian affiliate reportedly received more than 200 shipments of restricted Nvidia technology between June 2024 and June 2025. Megaspeed says inspections found no violations. The case moved to the Justice Department after Nvidia's general counsel emailed the agents in August 2025. No violation has been established.
  • The gap that did get exposed: export-control rules track who owns a chip and where it ships, and renting GPU time in third countries can provide compute through neither route. The new rules of November 16 target that gap.

What to do in your business this week

  • Separate the case's three strands: an espionage allegation resting on anonymous sources, reported relationships that Huang admitted only in general terms, and an export-control record with documented volumes and proceedings and no established violation. Each strand is judged on its own evidence.
  • Treat export compliance as board-level risk: Nvidia formed a board committee for national security, export controls and global trade risk. If your business sells technology, rents compute or works with customers in third countries, that committee is the model to copy.
  • Review your customer chain: the Megaspeed case shows risk arriving through the customer's customer. Ask who uses your product at the end of the chain and which controls document that use.
  • Watch the December 1 and 2 briefing: Huang and general counsel Tim Teter will answer behind closed doors before the Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and Select Committee on China. That briefing is the next date with new facts on the calendar.

Note: this article summarizes Nvidia's board statement of November 8, 2026, the 8-K filing of November 12, the board statement of November 17 and coverage by NPR, The New York Times via The Next Web, The Guardian and Semafor. The espionage and chip-diversion allegations are allegations under investigation, with no published conclusion. The attorney general has said no conclusion has been reached. The House briefing moved from November 18 to December 1 and 2.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Jensen Huang resign?

Nvidia's board accepted his resignation as president, CEO and director on November 11, 2026, effective immediately, three days after Huang admitted an affair to his staff. Nvidia has given no official reason for the resignation.

Who runs Nvidia now?

CFO Colette Kress serves as interim CEO and Harvey C. Jones became the first board chair since the company's founding. The board said on November 17 that it does not intend to reinstate Huang as CEO.

What is Megaspeed?

Megaspeed is a Singapore-based AI cloud provider and one of Nvidia's largest customers. The Commerce Department has investigated since September 2024 whether it diverted Nvidia chips to China, and the Justice Department has conducted a criminal investigation of the company since November 2026. No violation has been established.

Is the espionage proven?

No. The claim that a Megaspeed executive is a Chinese intelligence asset rests on anonymous officials and an unpublished memo. The attorney general has said no conclusion has been reached, and China calls it speculation without foundation.
