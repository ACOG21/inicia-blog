Muse beat ChatGPT in 12 days: the lesson is distribution

By inicIA · September 23, 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: Muse, Meta's AI agent, beat ChatGPT in downloads and daily active users within its first 12 days (1.8M vs 1.3M downloads; 642K vs 231K daily users in the US, per Apptopia). The win came from distribution: Meta activated its Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp user base.

Less than two weeks ago Meta launched Muse, its artificial intelligence agent. Within its first twelve days on the market, it had already beaten ChatGPT in downloads and daily active users.

The numbers, per estimates from analytics firm Apptopia:

  • 1.8 million downloads vs. 1.3 million for ChatGPT in the same post-launch window (iOS, US and Canada, for an apples-to-apples comparison).
  • 642,000 daily active users in the United States, vs. 231,000 for ChatGPT at the same point in its timeline.
  • 2.8 million installs worldwide in twelve days.
  • From second place to #1 on the US App Store.

ChatGPT had years of head start. Muse erased it in twelve days.

The lesson runs deeper than the technology.

It won by showing up where people already are

Over 95% of Muse users also use Facebook. 63% use Instagram. Meta didn't go hunting for new users: it activated the ones it already had.

Same playbook as Threads, which passed 500 million monthly users by riding on Instagram and Facebook. And now Muse lives inside WhatsApp too.

Here's what almost nobody is seeing — and what your business needs to understand:

Distribution decides the winner.

Your business can have the best service in your area. Visibility is what makes it real. Meta understood this perfectly: it put its AI inside the apps people already open every day.

The question that matters for you: are you showing up where your customers already spend their time?

The warning that comes with the news

Amazon already blocked purchases through Muse. It detected an unauthorized AI agent operating on its platform and removed it for violating its terms of use.

Amazon has its own AI models and runs one of the most-used inference platforms on the internet. It has zero obligation to leave the door open for a competitor's agent. And there's a practical reason: if someone else's agent places a wrong order, Amazon is the one handling the complaint.

The plain-English version for your business:

If your business lives rented on someone else's platform, someone else decides whether you exist tomorrow.

Traffic, customers, and sales have to land at YOUR place: your database, your WhatsApp, your system. Everything else is borrowed — and borrowed can be taken back.

The future arrived, and it already has a price tag

Meta launched paid Muse plans — $20 and $100 a month — plus a business interface. Shopify and Stripe are already working on integrations so the assistant can handle payments and purchases inside their platforms.

And the market has voted: Meta's stock is on track to close one of its best months in over a decade.

The era of AI-agent-driven commerce has already begun. The question is whether, when it reaches your industry, your business is riding the wave or watching it from the shore.

(Honest note: these figures are Apptopia estimates — a third party with no access to Meta's internal data. Meta, when asked, shared no official numbers. The trend, either way, is clear.)
