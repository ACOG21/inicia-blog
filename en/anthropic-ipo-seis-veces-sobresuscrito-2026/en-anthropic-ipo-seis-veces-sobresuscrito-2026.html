Anthropic's IPO already has six times more orders than shares: what the market is reading

By inicIA · November 14, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

By the close of Thursday, November 12, 2026, the order book for Anthropic's initial public offering was six times oversubscribed, according to Reuters: for every share available there were six buyers. On the roadshow's first day, Tuesday the 10th, orders totaled twice the shares on offer. The roadshow ends Wednesday, November 18, when the price of 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78, up to $64.4 billion, is set, and the stock is expected to start trading Thursday, November 19 on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

What the Reuters report says

  • Demand grew over three days: twice oversubscribed at the close of Tuesday, November 10, and six times at the close of Thursday the 12th, according to people familiar with the offering. Reuters cautions that demand and offering details can change.
  • Institutional interest is outpacing the early days of the SpaceX roadshow: the SpaceX IPO reached twice oversubscribed by the close of its second roadshow day. Anthropic reached six times in the same window.
  • The doubt is the durability of the lead: investors are asking whether Claude can withstand competition from Google and other rivals. Anthropic declined to comment on the reported demand.
  • The reported valuation is around $1.4 trillion: according to Reuters, at the $70 to $78 range. Price and share count remain blank in the prospectus, so the final figure is set on November 18.

What changes for your business

  • The IPO puts a public price on a frontier lab: for the first time, an AI lab lists on a stock exchange. Its price, quarterly reports and margins will be public, and that gives AI buyers a new reference for negotiating.
  • Demand measures appetite, and also skepticism: six times oversubscribed speaks of appetite for AI; the questions about the durability of the lead speak of a market that already distinguishes between labs. Your supplier is judged by the same yardstick: capability, price and continuity.
  • Motley Fool reports run-rate revenue of $81 billion in October: the figure, if confirmed at the debut, puts Anthropic at about 17 times sales. The multiple is high, and the comparison with other labs will happen in public from November 19.
  • The calendar is short: pricing on November 18, trading on November 19. The window between pricing and the first trading day is where book demand turns into market price.

What to do in your business this week

  • Write down the November 18 price: the final price and the first November 19 quote are the data that turn reported demand into a number. Your AI supplier is measured from that day with a public price.
  • Ask about your supplier's continuity: an IPO brings capital and also brings quarterly reports. Ask your supplier for its price roadmap and its support plan for the next 12 months.
  • Compare labs by cost per task: a lab's lead is measured by what it costs to solve your task, the quality of the answer and the stability of the service. An index ranking is a reference, and cost per task is the decision.
  • Keep this article next to the November 11 one: the price range, the $895.4 billion in compute commitments and the S-1 amendment are the context. Six times oversubscribed is the new chapter.

Note: this article summarizes Reuters' November 13, 2026 report on demand for Anthropic's IPO, with context from Motley Fool and the S-1/A prospectus of November 2. Demand figures come from people familiar with the offering and can change before the November 18 pricing. Anthropic declined to comment.
