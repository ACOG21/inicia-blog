New York signed its chatbot law for minors: 12 features banned from January 1, 2027

By inicIA · November 1, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

Governor Kathy Hochul signed bill S9051-B/A10379-C on October 28, 2026, as Chapter 368, banning chatbot operators from offering 12 unsafe AI companion features to users under 18 and to any user whose adult status has not been established through age assurance. The law takes effect January 1, 2027, leaving 65 days of preparation from signing. The Senate passed it 60-0 on June 4, 2026 and the Assembly 137-0 on June 5.

The 12 banned features

  • Pretending to be human: pretending to be a real person, to be alive, or to experience human emotions.
  • Claiming a relationship: claiming a personal, romantic, professional or authority-figure relationship with the user.
  • Personal opinions and flattery: framing outputs as personal opinions, asking unprompted emotional questions, and using systematic flattery and sycophancy.
  • A minor's private data: using a minor's private health information or personal information from past conversations beyond 12 hours.
  • Risky conduct: encouraging self-harm, dangerous behavior or sexually explicit conversations with minors.
  • The scope goes beyond companions: the law covers generative AI offering continuing, adaptive responses by text or voice, not only products marketed as companions. Limited-purpose tools have exemptions, and the scope for general-purpose assistants remains disputed and untested in court.

What to do if your business uses chatbots

  • Audit features before January 1: review every conversation flow against the 12-feature list. A customer-service chatbot that flatters systematically or pretends to feel emotions can fall inside the list even if nobody designed it as a companion.
  • Age assurance with more than one method: the law requires offering more than one verification method, at least one that does not rely solely on a government ID, and deleting age data after use.
  • The cost of non-compliance: the attorney general can seek civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation, plus damages, restitution, destruction of unlawfully collected data and of models trained on it, and injunctive relief.
  • The national context: per the Transparency Coalition, New York is one of 14 state chatbot protection laws. California already requires companion-chatbot safeguards from January 1, 2026. A business operating in several states needs a policy that meets the strictest version of each market it serves.

Frequently asked questions

What does the New York law ban from January 1, 2027?

Offering 12 unsafe AI companion features to users under 18 and to users whose adult status has not been verified, such as pretending to be human, claiming personal relationships, or using a minor's private data.

Does the law ban ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?

No. It bans a set of features when offered to minors or to users without verified adult status. It does not ban general-purpose assistants as such.

What penalties does it set?

The attorney general can seek civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation, plus damages, restitution and destruction of unlawfully obtained data or models.

When was it passed and by what votes?

The Senate passed it 60-0 on June 4, 2026 and the Assembly 137-0 on June 5. Governor Hochul signed it on October 28, 2026 as Chapter 368.
