China Took Back Meta's $2B Purchase and Manus Raised $500M: The Saga Explaining the Agent Market | inicIA

By inicIA · October 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

On October 8, 2026, Manus — the AI agent startup — announced it had raised over $500 million led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital, with Tencent, HSG, and ZhenFund participating. The round comes six months after China ordered the unwinding of its ~$2 billion sale to Meta, and targets a valuation near $4 billion (per Bloomberg; the company did not officially confirm it). It is the wildest story of the year in enterprise AI — and it leaves three lessons every business should understand about the agent era.

The saga, step by step

  • March 2025: Manus launches its general-purpose agent in invite-only beta (research, reports, website building with minimal input). It positions itself as the Chinese rival to OpenAI's $200/month agent; invite codes reportedly resell for the equivalent of over $1.3 million.
  • Mid-2025: the company moves its headquarters and team to Singapore and shuts most of its China operations.
  • December 2025: it reaches $100 million in annual recurring revenue — eight months after launch — and Meta announces its acquisition for over $2 billion to fold the technology into Meta AI.
  • March–April 2026: the co-founders face travel restrictions and Beijing summons them for questioning. On April 27, the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) orders the purchase unwound and bars foreign investment in Manus. Meta had already wired the money and integrated engineers.
  • June 2026: an internal memo reported by Bloomberg describes Meta "sunsetting" Manus inside its systems.
  • August 11, 2026: Manus announces it is operating independently again — the process included deleting certain user data.
  • October 2026: the $500M+ round, the launch of Manus 2.0 (new Cascade architecture), and of Cue, a personal-agent app with its own email, phone number, digital wallet, and computer.

Lesson 1: in AI, not even a closed deal is closed

A regulator voiding an already-paid, already-integrated acquisition has little precedent in tech. For your business, the read is not about China: it is about concentration risk. If your operation depends on a single agent provider or a single platform, a regulatory move, an acquisition, or a terms change can leave you without infrastructure overnight. Diversifying AI providers is no longer paranoia: it is business continuity.

Lesson 2: the money still believes in agents

$100M ARR in eight months, $2B offered by Meta, $500M raised after the worst possible regulatory scenario, and a $4B valuation target. Investors are not scared by foundation-model competition or the price war: they are betting that agents — software that executes complete tasks — are the layer where value gets captured. When smart capital bets like that, the question for your company is not whether agents arrive, but how fast.

Lesson 3: your next "customers" will have their own wallets

Cue gives the agent its own email, phone number, digital wallet, and computer, within limits the user sets. That means agents will buy from your business directly: booking, paying, hiring. Last week, Sierra and Meta launched the Personal Agent Protocol so businesses can identify those agents. Agentic-commerce infrastructure is being built in real time — on the agent side (Manus, Cue) and on the business side (protocols, permissions, APIs).

What to do this week

  1. Don't bet on a single agent provider: evaluate at least two alternatives for your critical flows.
  2. Prepare your business to receive agent-buyers: machine-readable catalog, clear pricing, tidy APIs or endpoints.
  3. Follow the money: when a category raises $500M after a regulatory block, that signals where competition will be in 18 months.

The industry's wild sagas are entertainment; the lessons they leave are strategy.

Frequently asked questions

What is Manus?

A general-purpose AI agent startup (research, process automation, task execution with minimal intervention), founded in China in 2022 by Butterfly Effect and moved to Singapore in 2025.

Why did China block the sale to Meta?

On April 27, 2026, the NDRC ordered the ~$2 billion acquisition announced in December 2025 unwound, barring foreign investment in Manus after a months-long probe — even though the company was already operating from Singapore.

How much did Manus raise in October 2026?

Over $500 million, led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital, with Tencent, HSG, and ZhenFund participating. Reports cite a $4B valuation target, not officially confirmed.

What is Cue?

Manus's new personal-agent app: it gives the agent its own email, phone number, digital wallet, and computer to communicate, execute tasks across services, and make payments within user-defined limits.
