Perplexity launched Teammates: the agent that keeps the job, not the question

By inicIA · November 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 4, 2026, Perplexity launched Teammates: AI agents that take on ongoing work —triaging email, tracking competitors, answering support— and carry it across sessions, instead of waiting for the next question. It is a research preview, web-only, for individual subscribers of the Max plan ($200 a month or $2,000 a year). Free and Pro plans do not include it, and Enterprise Pro and Enterprise Max do not include it by default. The iOS app comes later.

What makes a Teammate different

  • It remembers the work, not just the conversation: each Teammate keeps the project's goals, task history and shared files across sessions, and the feedback you give carries forward to the next run.
  • It coordinates other agents: a Teammate breaks a goal into tasks, hands the ones that can run in parallel to individual agents, and returns the finished work for human review.
  • It watches your tools: it can monitor connected tools such as Slack, GitHub and Linear, and works in its own isolated environment, with separate browser sessions.
  • The model is chosen per agent: new Teammates start on Claude Opus 5.5 by default, and the model can be changed per Teammate or per task.
  • The price is the plan, not the agent: Teammates are not sold separately. Access comes only with Max, which includes 10,000 Computer credits a month plus a one-time 35,000-credit bonus. Teammate usage is free during a promotional period and then draws on the plan's credits.

What it means for a business

  • The shift is from task to role: until now, an agent solved one request and shut down. A Teammate takes a role —for example, triaging the inbox— and stays there week after week. That is what brings it close to a coworker.
  • The cost is measured in credits, not subscriptions: every parallel run, every large file and every frequent automation consumes credits. Before assigning a role, calculate what a normal month of that work costs.
  • Human review stays in the middle: Perplexity says the Teammate returns finished work for review. That is the right design: the agent proposes and executes within limits, and a person approves what goes out.
  • There are no independent tests yet: no public reliability data, success rates or named customers. Perplexity's figure —that its staff built about 10,000 Teammates in the last 30 days— is company-reported, with no independent verification. Try it on a small job before handing it a critical one.

Note: this article summarizes Perplexity's November 4, 2026 announcement, its help center and its November 6 changelog, with coverage from TechRepublic and analysis from XenoSpectrum, i-scoop and other outlets. Teammates is a research preview and its behavior may change. No independent tests of its reliability have been published.
