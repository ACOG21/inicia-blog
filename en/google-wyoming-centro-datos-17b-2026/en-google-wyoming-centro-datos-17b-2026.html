Google invests $17 billion in its first Wyoming data center: 702 MW fuel cells with carbon capture

By inicIA · November 6, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 5, 2026, Google announced a long-term $17 billion investment in its first Wyoming data center, in Laramie County, alongside Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy, American Electric Power (AEP) and Related Digital. Power will come from a 702 MW fuel-cell development with carbon capture and storage (CCS), contracted with AEP: the first of its kind at commercial scale and the largest planned to date, per Google. The fuel cells, manufactured in the United States by Bloom Energy, convert natural gas into electricity, and the captured carbon would be stored at Tallgrass's sequestration hub in eastern Wyoming. Google will pay for 100% of the electricity it uses and cover the infrastructure its growth directly requires. The data center itself still has no published construction schedule or opening date.

The numbers that matter

  • $17 billion investment: Google's long-term investment in Wyoming, its first data center in the state, with hundreds of millions of dollars in local property taxes per the coverage.
  • 702 MW of fuel cells with CCS: the development contracted with AEP, the largest of its kind planned to date. With this contract, Google's total capacity tied to carbon capture reaches nearly 1 gigawatt.
  • ~$4 billion from AEP in the plant: AEP plans to invest about $4 billion in the fuel-cell facility under a 20-year power purchase agreement with Google, with construction from 2026 and full operation expected by the end of 2028.
  • Second half of 2027, power service start: per Black Hills' disclosures, power service would start in the second half of 2027, with peak load expected in 2030.
  • Air-cooled: the data center will use air cooling to reduce pressure on local water, with water-replenishment investments planned. Google's June 2026 water stewardship policy notes that water cooling can cut energy use by about 10% against air cooling, so the choice carries an energy cost.

What it means for your business

  • Energy is now the project, not an input: the Wyoming announcement gives as much space to electricity as to the building. If your business depends on compute capacity, the future price of AI includes generation, carbon capture and community negotiation, not just GPU rental.
  • Same day, same message: Google announced Wyoming the same day OpenAI published its community compact, two days after elections in which 40 of 41 local data-center measures went against development. The big labs already compete on who pays for their electricity and who publishes their data.
  • Announced and signed are two different things: the AEP contract went public in October and the Black Hills contract too, before Google's announcement. The lesson for buying infrastructure: the press release is the frame; the contracts and their dates are the real commitment.
  • Carbon capture still has no published rate: Google says it will capture most of the CO2, without giving a specific rate, and the announcement gives no delivery schedule for the data center. A $17 billion project is judged by verifiable milestones, not by the size of the headline.

Note: this article summarizes Google's November 5, 2026 announcement (official blog) and coverage from Unite.AI, Securities.io, DealRoom and XenoSpectrum. AEP's investment figures, operating schedules and property taxes are reported from AEP and Black Hills disclosures. Google has not published a construction schedule or opening date for the data center, or a specific CO2 capture rate.
