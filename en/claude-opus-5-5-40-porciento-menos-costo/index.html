Claude Opus 5.5 costs 40% less: what it means for you

By inicIA · September 23, 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: Anthropic launched Claude Opus 5.5 on September 22, 2026, the first model in its 5.5 family. It performs at the level of its flagship model (Fable 5.1) on most tasks, but costs 40% less to run than its predecessor and generates responses 30% faster. For businesses, the message is clear: frontier AI is becoming a commodity, and automating keeps getting cheaper.

Anthropic just made a move that changes the economics of artificial intelligence: Claude Opus 5.5.

The hard numbers from the launch:

  • 40% cheaper to run than Opus 5 on typical workloads.
  • API pricing: $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens (previously $5 and $25).
  • Cache reads — which Anthropic says account for most of the cost in agent workloads — dropped from $0.50 to $0.20 per million (60% less).
  • Over 30% faster at generating responses; a fast mode reaches up to 2.5x speed.
  • Performs at the level of Claude Fable 5.1 — its priciest model ($10/$50 per million) — on most work.

And it's about more than price. In coding, an early tester audited and fixed a 200,000-line codebase in under 3 hours; Opus 5 took 20+ hours and 2.5x the tokens. In internal testing it beats competing flagship models at a fraction of the cost per task.

The real story goes beyond the model. It's the trend.

Every six months, frontier AI costs substantially less. What was a lab luxury last year now sells for pennies per million tokens.

This has a direct consequence for your business that almost nobody is calculating:

Every month you wait for AI to "mature," automating gets cheaper for you... and for your competition too.

The edge belongs to whoever builds their systems now, when the cost is already negligible, and lets each new generation make them more powerful without rebuilding anything.

Think of it this way: if automating your customer service, follow-ups, or quotes cost $100 a month in compute last year and costs $60 today with better quality, what will it cost in 2027? The question to ask: can you afford to let your competition get there first?
