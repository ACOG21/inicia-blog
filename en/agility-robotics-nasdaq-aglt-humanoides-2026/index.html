Agility Robotics hit Nasdaq: the first listed humanoid already works in real warehouses

By inicIA · November 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 5, 2026, Agility Robotics began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker AGLT (warrants AGLTW), after completing its merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI. The deal and its related financings represent about $465 million in gross capital and a pre-money transaction valuation of about $2.5 billion, according to Bloomberg. Agility describes itself as the first US-listed pure-play humanoid robotics company with proven commercial deployments: its Digit robot moves totes in customer warehouses, and its new model, Digit 5, already has more than $300 million in committed multi-year orders.

The numbers behind the listing

  • The path was not a traditional IPO: Agility went public through a SPAC. Churchill XI shareholders approved the merger on November 2 with more than 84% of the votes, and the merger closed on November 4.
  • $465 million in gross capital: about $164 million from the SPAC trust, a $201 million private investment (PIPE) led by Foxconn at $10 a share, and $100 million in equity received before closing. The $100 million arrived earlier, so it is not new money from debut day.
  • Valuation of about $2.5 billion: that is the pre-money transaction valuation reported by Bloomberg, not the market capitalization, which moves with the share price.
  • More than $300 million in Digit 5 orders: these are committed multi-year orders, subject to contractual milestones, product features and specifications. They are not realized revenue.
  • Digit 5 arrived in September 2026: early customer access is expected in the first half of 2027, with general availability targeted for the end of that year.

What Digit actually does

  • One concrete job: Digit is a bipedal humanoid robot designed to move totes and containers in warehouses, factories and distribution centers, alongside human workers, on repetitive and physically demanding tasks.
  • Almost three years of real deployment: the company reports more than 65,000 hours of operation and customers such as GXO Logistics, Schaeffler and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada. AGCO signed a commercial pilot expected to begin in 2027.
  • Its own manufacturing: Agility built RoboFab, a plant in Salem, Oregon, to produce robots at scale instead of hand-assembling them in batches.
  • Heavyweight investors: its strategic investors and partners include NVIDIA, Amazon, SoftBank and Foxconn.

Why it matters for a business that does not build robots

  • Physical AI now has a market price: until now, humanoids lived in demos and private balance sheets. With Agility listed, every quarter it will publish revenue, deployments and production. The sector gets its first public benchmark.
  • Repetitive work is the first market: Digit does not try to replace a whole worker, but to take the task that repeats most and wears people out. That is the same logic a business uses to adopt a software agent: the repetitive work first, then the complex work.
  • The question is scale, not the demo: Agility's challenge is to go from dozens of robots in pilots to hundreds in daily operation. For anyone evaluating physical automation, Agility's quarterly reports will be the best source of real sector data.
  • Committed orders are not sales: the $300 million for Digit 5 depends on the robot meeting milestones and specifications. A conditional commitment is a sign of interest, not revenue.

Note: this article summarizes Agility Robotics' November 5, 2026 announcement and coverage from TechRepublic, Securities.io and The Robot Report. The $2.5 billion valuation is a pre-money transaction valuation reported by Bloomberg, not market capitalization.
