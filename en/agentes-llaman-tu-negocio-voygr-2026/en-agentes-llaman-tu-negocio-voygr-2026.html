Your business will soon get calls from other people's agents: 848 calls in San Francisco preview it

By inicIA · November 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

During San Francisco Tech Week in early October 2026, startup VOYGR used its PlaceCall service to have an AI agent call 848 San Francisco businesses across 13 categories, including vets, dentists, bars, cafes and salons. Per coverage of The San Francisco Standard, picked up by The Bay Area Brief and The Cooldown, the agent identified itself as an AI on every call, called only during business hours, skipped chains and mobile numbers, and asked one short question, such as whether a dentist was taking new patients. About 1 in 3 businesses kept talking after learning it was an AI, and 1 in 25 calls was answered by another AI, mostly at restaurants, bars and dental offices.

The numbers that matter

  • 848 calls, 13 categories: the experiment measured willingness to talk, with service and pricing questions such as a salon's cheapest haircut or a dentist's availability.
  • 79% of vet offices kept talking: the most receptive category, followed by dentists at 61% per experiment coverage. Bars and dry cleaners, near 20%, were most likely to hang up.
  • 1 in 25 already had AI on the phone: some of the businesses reached already use their own automated system for inbound calls.
  • 96% of target businesses earn under $1 million a year: the experiment focused on small businesses, per coverage, so the results describe that segment, not the whole economy.

What to prepare in your business before you get called

  • A script for inbound agents: which information your team may give by phone (hours, general availability, published prices) and which is never confirmed without identity verification, such as customer data or payments.
  • Written permissions for bookings and purchases: an inbound agent may request a reservation or a quote. Define what can be closed by phone and what needs human confirmation or customer payment.
  • A time and retry limit: agent calls may repeat to verify data. Define how many times the same query is handled and which channel the next one goes to.
  • A call log: date, question, answer given, and whether the caller identified as an AI. That log protects the business when a booking or price is disputed later.
  • Test your own auto-attendant: if your business already uses AI on the phone, check that an inbound agent can complete a simple task without getting stuck in a loop.

Note: this article summarizes coverage of The San Francisco Standard on VOYGR's experiment during October 2026 Tech Week, picked up by The Bay Area Brief, The Cooldown and WiseVoter. The figures come from one company's experiment with small businesses in one city, and describe willingness to talk, not a representative survey of all businesses or proof that any particular agent closes sales.
