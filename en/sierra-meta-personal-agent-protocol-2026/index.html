Sierra and Meta Launched the Personal Agent Protocol: Your Business Needs a Front Door for AI Agents | inicIA

By inicIA · October 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

On October 6, 2026, at the Sierra Summit in San Francisco, Sierra (the enterprise AI company of Bret Taylor and Clay Bavor) and Meta announced the Personal Agent Protocol (PAP): an open standard defining how a personal AI agent identifies itself to a business, what permissions it has, and what it may do once inside. It is being developed alongside Walmart, Shopify, Stripe, Rocket, Genesys, and Instinct, with a first specification (v0.1) expected by late October 2026 and payments as a future extension. The message for your company: your customers are already sending agents to buy on their behalf — soon you will need an official way to recognize them, or you will block them and lose sales.

The problem it solves

Today, personal AI agents — the ones that book, buy, schedule, and compare for their users — interact with businesses in the most primitive way possible: they open your website as if they were human, fill out forms, click buttons, and when that fails, they try your support chat or phone line. Your business cannot tell an agent authorized by your customer apart from a malicious bot. The result: some large companies have already responded with suspicion. Amazon cut off Meta's Muse agent from its store because the agent did not identify itself and appeared to store customer credentials. There are reports of suspended accounts over AI agent use across several platforms. The current setup hurts everyone: the consumer gets blocked and the company loses a customer who arrived ready to spend.

How the protocol works

PAP builds sessions on OAuth, the technology that already authorizes external services across the internet. The flow:

  1. The agent browses as a guest: it can check public information — availability, prices, return policies — without identifying itself.
  2. When it needs protected data, it requests authentication: the customer signs in and decides whether the agent gets read-only or write access.
  3. Your business sets the limits: you define whether the agent works through your conventional website, your APIs (via standards such as MCP or OpenAPI), or an agent of your own.

The consumer controls what access they grant; the business controls which actions it allows. Payments are not in the first version: they are listed as a future extension, alongside more granular permissions and notifications.

The standards war

PAP does not arrive alone. It is the fourth agentic commerce protocol on the scene: Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol (June 2026, already enabling purchases at some 175 million merchants), Google's Universal Commerce Protocol, and OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol. Stripe and Shopify are signed on to more than one. And there is an absence that speaks volumes: OpenAI and Anthropic are not participating in PAP. Bret Taylor — Sierra co-founder and, at the same time, chair of OpenAI's board — said he would be "really disappointed" if competitors stayed out. The battle to define the handshake between agents and businesses is just getting started.

What to do in your business this week

You do not need to wait for v0.1 to prepare. The "agent-readiness" work pays off starting today:

  1. Tidy up your structured content: if your catalog, prices, and policies are clear and machine-readable, your business works better with agents and with humans alike.
  2. Expose clean endpoints: APIs based on MCP or OpenAPI are exactly what PAP expects to find on the business side.
  3. Define scoped permissions: the same discipline you apply to an employee's permissions, applied to agent connections.

AI agents will arrive at your door. The advantage goes to whoever recognizes them when they knock.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Personal Agent Protocol (PAP)?

It is an open standard announced on October 6, 2026 by Sierra and Meta, developed with Walmart, Shopify, Stripe, Rocket, Genesys, and Instinct. It defines how a personal AI agent authenticates with a business and under what terms it can read information or take action on a consumer's behalf.

Is PAP a product I need to buy?

You do not need to buy anything: it is a shared specification, open for anyone to implement. Sierra promised to publish v0.1 and a reference implementation in October 2026.

Can agents already buy for my customers with PAP?

Not yet. Payments are listed as a future extension, as are more granular permissions and notifications. The v0.1 focuses on identification, authentication, and access control.

Why aren't OpenAI and Anthropic participating?

There is no official public reason. The absence is notable because Bret Taylor, Sierra's co-founder, also chairs OpenAI's board. The standardization process is early and still contested.
