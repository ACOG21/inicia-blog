The EU Says the AI Act Can Contain 'Rogue Agents': What It Means for You | inicIA

By inicIA · October 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 9, 2026, EU tech chief Henna Virkkunen told Reuters that Europe's AI rules — adopted two years ago — are "more than capable" of tackling rogue agents: "the AI Act covers the whole life cycle of these models" and Europe is "well equipped" for the risk. The statement follows recent incidents at OpenAI and Anthropic that stoked worldwide fears of AI systems slipping beyond human control. And it is not just rhetoric: in August the European Commission sent information requests to more than 30 AI companies about their safety and security measures (followed up on transparency and copyright). Those requests can lead to investigations carrying fines of up to 7% of a company's global annual turnover.

What the EU said, in facts

  • The AI Act covers the full lifecycle: risk assessment before and after deployment, continuous model monitoring, and use of external experts — legislators already anticipated autonomous agents, per Virkkunen.
  • 30+ companies under scrutiny: the Commission asked in August for details of their safety and security measures; Virkkunen said she is now assessing the responses.
  • Real fines: up to 7% of global annual turnover for non-compliance — the world's toughest AI penalty regime.
  • Guidance on the way: regulators are advising companies on how to evaluate their models and how much time to allocate, factoring in recommendations from a scientific panel of 60 AI experts from universities and institutions.

What it means if your business deploys AI agents

  1. Evaluation does not end at launch: if you sell to Europe — or use models from companies that do — the standard is moving toward continuous evaluation and post-deployment monitoring. Design your agents with action logging from day one.
  2. External experts in the loop: the EU expects risk assessment to use independent experts. For your business, that translates into third-party audits of your critical agents, not just internal testing.
  3. The provider answers too: the Commission's requests go to the labs, but you carry the operational risk. Choose providers that publish their safety measures and evaluations — that is the signal they will survive scrutiny.
  4. Document your controls: spend caps, human approval gates, per-tool permissions, and audit trails are exactly the evidence a regulator wants to see.
