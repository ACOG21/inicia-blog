Meta Muse Now Works for Your Business: Instagram, Ads, and WhatsApp on Assisted Autopilot | inicIA

By inicIA · October 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

On September 29, 2026, Meta announced Muse for Small Business: its personal Muse agent now connects to your Facebook and Instagram business accounts, your ad account, and tools like Shopify, QuickBooks, Slack, Canva, Stripe, and Zoom. The agent analyzes sales and campaigns, reviews expenses, drafts ads and customer messages, and helps plan growth. The golden rule, in Meta's words: nothing gets published, sent, or spent without your approval. Available in the US and Canada. It is not autopilot: it is an assistant with superpowers that always asks your permission.

What it can do for your business

Per Meta's announcement, Muse for Small Business helps owners run daily operations across six fronts: advertising, sales, social media, finance, creative work, and customer communication. Concretely: it analyzes which ads and content are working, flags unusual expenses, surfaces emails that need replies and drafts responses, and builds growth plans with your business context.

The difference from a chatbot: Muse executes tasks with your connected tools —it opens, reviews, drafts, organizes— instead of just answering questions.

The connections that matter

The announced connector list includes Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe, and Zoom, plus your professional Facebook and Instagram accounts and your Meta ad account. Zapier joined the same day as a connector, opening the door to 9,000+ apps and 40,000 actions. And if your tool is not on the list, Meta says you can build custom connectors.

Practical translation: if your business already lives on Shopify + QuickBooks + Instagram, Muse can see all three at once and work with that context. That is what makes it useful: not the model, but the access to your data.

The limit you must know

Two honest limits. First: it is an assistant, not a replacement. Meta repeats that nothing gets published, sent, or spent without your approval — which is a safeguard, but also means the final work is still yours: review, approve, decide. Second: do not confuse Meta's two agents. Muse (launched September 8 for US consumers) works for the customer: it books, buys, and negotiates for them. Meta Business Agent (launched June 3 globally) works for the business: it answers, recommends, and sells for you on WhatsApp, Messenger, and ads. Meta is selling an agent to each side of the conversation — and charging on both.

What to do before connecting anything

  1. Access inventory. Decide which accounts you connect and with what permissions. An agent with access to your ads and your books is powerful: give it only what it needs for its job.
  2. Define what you approve. Approval cannot be an automatic "yes" to everything. Define which decisions always go through you (publishing, spending, replying to upset customers).
  3. Start with one task. Campaign analysis or message drafts, for example. Measure the time it saves before handing it more keys.
  4. Consider your own agent. Muse is generic and lives inside Meta's ecosystem. If your operation needs specific business rules, your own brand voice, and full data control, a custom agent (like the ones inicIA builds) plays in a different league: your business's, not the platform's.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Muse for Small Business cost?

Meta says most uses are free, with subscription plans for heavy use. As a reference, the personal Muse plans are Power ($20/month) and Maximum ($100/month) as TechCrunch reported at launch.

Does it work outside the US and Canada?

Not yet: the launch covers the United States and Canada. Meta has announced no international dates.

Can Muse see my passwords or share my data with Meta's ads?

Per Meta, no: Muse has no visibility into passwords or payment methods, and does not share your conversations or your private machine's data with its ad systems. A separate agent (Sentinel) must approve everything Muse sends to the internet.

Does it replace my marketing or sales team?

No: it amplifies. It drafts, analyzes data, and organizes work, but strategy, your brand voice, and final decisions stay human.
