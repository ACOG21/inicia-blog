Connecticut Became the Strictest US State for AI in Business on Oct 1 | inicIA

By inicIA · October 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 1, 2026, the first sections of Connecticut's Artificial Intelligence Responsibility and Transparency Act (CART Act) — passed as SB 5 and signed June 2, 2026 — took effect, alongside the AI-linked layoff law (SB 7, Public Act 26-15). What already applies to your business if you operate in Connecticut: (1) if you issue a mass-layoff notice under the federal WARN Act, you must declare whether the decision was related to your use of artificial intelligence; (2) using an automated system is no defense against employment discrimination claims in Connecticut; (3) disclosure duties for AI subscriptions and synthetic-content provenance (Section 15) kicked in. The full notice regime for automated employment decision tools (AEDT) — which tool you use, for what purpose, and what data it analyzes — applies from October 1, 2027.

What changed on October 1, in facts

  • WARN notice with an AI declaration (SB 7): any employer notifying the Connecticut Department of Labor of a mass layoff or closing covered by federal WARN must now state whether the action was "related to the employer's use of artificial intelligence or another technological change." Connecticut is among the first states in the country to require it.
  • AI is no defense against discrimination: the CART Act amends Connecticut's Fair Employment Practices Act to make clear that attributing a challenged decision to an automated system does not shield you from liability. If the tool produces a discriminatory outcome, the employer is still liable.
  • Synthetic content provenance: providers generating synthetic digital content (images, video, audio, text) face transparency obligations effective October 1.
  • Chatbots and companion technologies: safety protocols, parental controls, and restrictions on interactions with minors.

What arrives October 1, 2027 (and why to prepare now)

The core of the employment regime — AEDT notices — applies to systems deployed on or after Oct 1, 2027: employers must give prior written notice to applicants and employees when an automated system makes or materially influences an employment decision, identifying the tool by trade name, its purpose, and the categories and sources of personal data it analyzes. Employers must also disclose when a person interacts directly with the system. Covered decisions include hiring, promotion, compensation, discipline, scheduling, and termination. Courts may weigh in your favor documented anti-bias testing or similar proactive efforts.

The checklist for your business

  1. Tool inventory: list everything using AI in personnel decisions — resume filters, candidate ranking, performance analytics, monitoring — even if the vendor calls it an "assistant."
  2. Decide who signs the AI-causation call: the WARN duty requires determining whether AI "substantially caused or contributed to" a layoff. Define today who makes that call and on what evidence — not mid-restructuring.
  3. Review your candidate notices: get the 2027 work done early — tool trade name, purpose, data analyzed — and use it as a transparency advantage now.
  4. Documented bias testing: if you use these tools, run and document anti-bias tests. The law explicitly rewards them.

Frequently asked questions

What is Connecticut's CART Act?

The Artificial Intelligence Responsibility and Transparency Act (SB 5, signed June 2, 2026): one of the most comprehensive US state AI frameworks, with 39 sections covering employment, synthetic content, chatbots, a regulatory sandbox, and public procurement, on a staggered timeline.

What does the WARN notice with AI require?

Since Oct 1, 2026 (SB 7, Public Act 26-15), an employer issuing a mass-layoff or closing notice under federal WARN must declare in that notice whether the decision was related to its use of artificial intelligence or another technological change.

Does AI protect me if it discriminates?

No. The CART Act makes clear that using an automated system is no defense against discrimination claims under Connecticut law. Courts may, however, weigh documented anti-bias testing in your favor.

When do the full AEDT notices apply?

October 1, 2027, for systems deployed on or after that date: prior written notice with the tool's trade name, purpose, and categories of data analyzed.
