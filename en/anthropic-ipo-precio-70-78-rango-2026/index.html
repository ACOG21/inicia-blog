Anthropic priced its IPO: $70 to $78 a share and up to $64.4B at stake

By inicIA · November 11, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 10, 2026, Anthropic filed the second amendment to its prospectus (S-1/A) and launched the roadshow for its initial public offering: 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78, raising up to $64.4 billion at the top of the range. Of those shares, 801.3 million are sold by the company and 23.6 million by existing stockholders. In parallel, Anthropic agreed a $26 billion private placement of Class N shares, at the IPO price, with seven partners: AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC. At the top of the range, Reuters calculates a valuation near $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis. The shares would trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

The draft that circulated and was retracted the night before

  • On the night of November 9, Anthropic sent banks a draft prospectus with different numbers: about 866.7 million shares at $70 to $80, up to $69.3 billion. Hours later, a follow-up email said the pricing details were inaccurate and that the company would update the filing, according to Reuters, which reviewed the emails.
  • The version filed on November 10 is smaller: about 42 million fewer shares and $2 less at the top of the range, about $5 billion less in maximum proceeds. The $26 billion private placement stayed the same.
  • What this teaches: even the largest AI IPO in history had a draft with figures the company itself corrected within hours. For your business, the lesson is the same: a number circulating in an email is not a confirmed number. The filing with the SEC is the source that counts.
  • Reported dates: according to CNBC, pricing would be set on November 18 and trading would begin on November 19. The Wall Street Journal reported Anthropic hopes trading begins November 19 or 20. These are reported dates, not confirmed by the company in the prospectus.

Why the private placement matters more than the price

  • The supply chain buys in alongside you: the seven private-placement partners are the chip makers and clouds that sell compute to Anthropic. When your suppliers become your shareholders, your ability to get chips and capacity stops depending on a waiting line.
  • Total fundraising can reach $90.4 billion adding the IPO and the private placement, according to Reuters. It is the largest public offering Wall Street has seen from an AI lab.
  • The prospectus warns about its own risks: competitors may develop models faster, and advances in AI may outpace the company's safety measures. Those warnings are in the filed document, and an AI buyer should read them with the same attention as the revenue figures.
  • The revenue context: Anthropic reported $16.3 billion in revenue in the first half of 2026 in its October S-1, with an $80.7 billion loss in the same period, according to the prospectus. The company also reported $895.4 billion in compute commitments in its November 2 amendment.

What to do with this news if you buy AI

  • Your vendor's price is a stability signal: a listed company publishes its accounts every quarter. That transparency is a tool for judging whether your vendor will still exist in three years.
  • Dependence on compute is the real risk: $895.4 billion in compute commitments is a figure that explains why Anthropic needs so much capital. Ask your vendor how it secures its capacity before signing a long contract.
  • Read the full prospectus: the $1.4 trillion valuation is on a fully diluted basis and includes options and restricted stock. The comparable figure depends on the calculation method, so compare carefully.
  • The IPO does not change your contract today: Claude's prices and terms for your business do not move because of a roadshow. What changes is the public information available to judge the vendor.

Note: this article summarizes Anthropic's S-1/A amendment of November 10, 2026 and coverage in Reuters, CNBC and The Hindu BusinessLine. Share and price figures are from the document filed with the SEC. The $1.4 trillion valuation is Reuters' calculation on a fully diluted basis; other methods give different figures. Pricing and trading dates are reported by CNBC and the Wall Street Journal, not confirmed by the company.
