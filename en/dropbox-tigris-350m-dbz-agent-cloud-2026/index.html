Dropbox paid $350M for Tigris Data: storage for AI agents is now a business

By inicIA · November 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 5, 2026, Dropbox announced it will acquire Tigris Data for about $350 million in cash, in what appears to be the largest acquisition in its history since it was founded in 2007. The definitive agreement was signed on November 3, and Dropbox expects to close the deal in the fourth quarter of 2026, if customary conditions are met. The purchase powers DBX Agent Cloud, an S3-compatible storage platform for companies and developers building AI models and agents.

What Dropbox is buying

  • Tigris Data, founded in 2021 in Sunnyvale, California, by former Uber engineers who helped build that company's storage infrastructure. Its product is distributed object storage designed for AI workloads.
  • A price 14 times its last round: Tigris had raised about $31.9 million since its founding, including a $25 million Series A in October 2025 led by Spark Capital, with Andreessen Horowitz and Basis Set Ventures.
  • Dropbox's previous largest deal was HelloSign, $230 million in 2019. DocSend cost $165 million in 2021 and FormSwift $95 million in 2022. The Tigris deal tops all three.
  • DBX Agent Cloud starts as stand-alone object storage that works with tools built for Amazon S3, aimed at businesses developing AI agents and models.
  • No headcount cuts announced: Dropbox told TechCrunch it has no plans to cut headcount as part of the acquisition.

Why a file company is buying storage for agents

  • Agents need memory and files: an agent that works for hours straight produces documents, logs, images and intermediate states. Someone has to store them, version them and serve them fast. That is the gap Dropbox wants to fill.
  • AI infrastructure is splitting into layers: models, compute, storage, identity and governance. Each layer becomes its own market, and established software companies are buying their way in instead of building from scratch.
  • The multiple says something about the market: paying $350 million for a company that raised $31.9 million is a bet that storage for agents will grow far faster than traditional file storage.
  • The lesson for AI buyers: when you evaluate an agent, ask where its files live, who controls them and what happens to them if you switch vendors. Storage is part of the cost and the risk, even if it never shows up in the demo.

Note: this article summarizes Dropbox's November 5, 2026 announcement and its SEC filing (Form 8-K), with coverage from TechCrunch, Crunchbase News and other outlets. The $350 million price is approximate and subject to customary adjustments; closing depends on customary conditions, and Dropbox warns it may not happen within the expected timeframe.

Frequently asked questions

How much did Dropbox pay for Tigris Data?

About $350 million in cash, under the definitive agreement signed November 3, 2026. It is Dropbox's largest acquisition since its founding in 2007, above the $230 million paid for HelloSign in 2019.

What is DBX Agent Cloud?

Dropbox's new storage platform for companies and developers building AI models and agents. It starts as S3-compatible object storage, so existing tools work with it.

When does the deal close?

Dropbox expects to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, if customary conditions are met. Until then, the deal is not closed.

Had Dropbox bought AI companies before?

Yes. It previously acquired Reclaim.ai, Promoted.ai, Mobius Labs and Nira, all AI-focused, but none approached the $350 million scale of Tigris.
