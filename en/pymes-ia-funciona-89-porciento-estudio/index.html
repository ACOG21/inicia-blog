89% of Businesses Using AI Say It Works (Saving $4,100/Year) | inicIA

By inicIA · October 6, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

The debate over whether AI "really works" for a small business has a data-backed answer: Homebase's 2026 Main Street AI Gap Report, published September 22 from a survey of 750 U.S. small business owners and operators, found that 89% of those using AI report a positive impact on their business. Those applying AI to scheduling and payroll together save an average of 7.7 hours and $343 a month —about $4,100 a year—. Adoption rose from 64% in 2025 to 74% in 2026. And there is a gap that should concern you: businesses at $5 million or more in revenue adopt AI at 81%, while those under $500,000 barely reach 54%. The smallest businesses —the ones with the least room for expensive mistakes— are the ones falling furthest behind.

The study's numbers

Key findings from the report, from the 750-owner survey:

  • 89% of AI adopters report an overall positive business impact, and 87% say it saves them time.
  • 74% already use or are piloting AI tools, a 10-point jump in a single year (64% in 2025).
  • $4,100 a year in savings: those using AI for both scheduling and payroll save 7.7 hours and $343 a month on average.
  • The size gap: 81% adoption at $5M+ revenue versus 54% under $500K. By headcount: 57% at 5–9 employees versus 84% at 50–99.
  • Non-users want in: 62% of non-adopters are still interested. What holds them back is not price: it is trust —data privacy (38%) and fear of mistakes (31%)—.
  • Growth, not cuts: owners are more than twice as likely to expect AI to help grow the business (49%) as to expect it to reduce headcount (22%).

As Homebase founder and CEO John Waldmann put it, more than one in ten owners surveyed said scheduling or payroll errors cost them $2,000 or more a month. AI is already giving them time and money back to focus on scaling.

What it means for your business

Three practical takeaways:

  1. ROI is no longer a bet. With 89% of users reporting positive impact, the question stopped being "does it work?" and became "where do I start?" The study's answer: with operational pain points —scheduling, payroll, follow-up— where savings are measurable from month one.
  2. The barrier is trust, not technology. If what holds you back is fear of mistakes or exposing data, the fix is a provider that answers in writing where your data goes, with human oversight from day one. That is contracted, not improvised.
  3. The size gap is your window. Small businesses adopt more slowly, which means acting now puts you ahead of few competitors. Every year you wait, that edge shrinks.

Where to start this week

You do not need a six-month project. The pattern among businesses reporting results is to start with one expensive, measurable pain point:

  • If you lose prospects by not answering fast: an agent that answers in minutes and books on its own.
  • If your team drowns in repetitive tasks: a follow-up agent that re-engages forgotten prospects.
  • If operational chaos eats your hours: pick one process (scheduling, collections, service) and automate it.

Measure before and after: hours saved, prospects answered, deals closed. The study's $4,100 is an average; your number comes from your figures.

Frequently asked questions

Is the study reliable?

It is a survey of 750 U.S. small business owners and operators, published by Homebase (an AI operator for small businesses) on September 22, 2026. Like any vendor study, read it with judgment, but the sample and methodology are transparent.

Does $4,100 a year justify investing in AI?

That is only the scheduling-and-payroll savings. Full return includes prospects previously lost to slow response and team hours redirected to selling. Run it with your numbers: monthly prospects times average ticket times close rate.

What if my business is very small?

All the more reason: the study shows small businesses adopt the least, so the competitive edge of acting now is bigger. Start with one agent on the most expensive pain point, not a full transformation.

What holds back those still not using AI?

Data privacy (38%) and fear of mistakes (31%). Both are solved with a serious provider: a written data contract and human oversight from day one.
