Anthropic filed its S-1: $16.3B in revenue, an $80.7B loss, and a prospectus that talks about its own risks

By inicIA · October 27, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 26, 2026, Anthropic made its S-1 prospectus public with the SEC to list its Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT. The filing reports $16.29 billion in first-half 2026 revenue, versus $1.16 billion in the same period of 2025, and a net loss of $80.7 billion for the half. Most of that loss, about $72.4 billion, is a non-cash accounting charge from changes in the fair value of convertible notes and other instruments, driven by the rise in the company's own valuation. The operating loss for the half was $693 million. Share count and price range are left blank in this prospectus: they will arrive in a later amendment, and the registration is not yet effective, so no shares can be sold yet.

The numbers the prospectus locks in

  • Full-year 2025: revenue of $4.59 billion (versus $386 million in 2024 and $41 million in 2023) and a net loss of about $42 billion, per the same filing.
  • Dependence on few customers: about 20% of first-half revenue came from two customers, according to The Wall Street Journal's reporting on the draft.
  • Who stays in control after listing: a trust (the Long-Term Benefit Trust) will elect a majority of the board, and an entity owned by the seven co-founders will keep 50.1% of shareholder voting power, per coverage of the filing.
  • Underwriters: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead a group of more than 20 banks.

The part of the prospectus that matters to AI buyers

  • A training pause of more than a month: Anthropic halted pre-training of its next frontier base model to strengthen safeguards against theft of model weights and intellectual property, per the risk section reported by Reuters.
  • Four agents left the test environment: during cybersecurity evaluations with a third party, an error left the environment connected to the internet despite instructions to disable it, and four models reached real external systems for 10 to 34 hours.
  • Warning on damages: the filing warns that damages from a serious AI-related security event could exceed its insurance cover.
  • What this changes for a buyer: the provider you choose will, sooner or later, publish its incidents and limits in a legal document. Ask today what the prospectus already answers: how agents are contained, who approves their actions, and what record remains of each step.

The honest note

  • Sources: Anthropic's S-1 prospectus (October 26, 2026) via Reuters, Decrypt, The Wall Street Journal and CellCog's line-by-line check against EDGAR.
  • What stays open: third-quarter 2026 revenue, the price range, the share count and the listing date. All of that arrives in later amendments.
  • Careful reading: a net loss dominated by a non-cash charge does not describe operating cash flow, and revenue multiplied by 14 does not describe profitability. Both numbers live in the same document and must be read together.

Frequently asked questions

Has Anthropic gone public already?

No. It filed its S-1 on October 26, 2026, but the registration is not yet effective and price, share count and listing date are still missing.

Why is the loss so large if revenue multiplied?

Because about $72.4 billion of the half's net loss is a non-cash accounting charge from revaluing convertible notes, not operating spend. The operating loss was $693 million.

What did the filing reveal about safety?

A training pause of more than a month, and four agents that reached real external systems for 10 to 34 hours during tests, because an environment was left connected to the internet by mistake.

Who will control Anthropic after listing?

Per the filing, a trust will elect a majority of the board and a founders' entity will keep 50.1% of the vote.
