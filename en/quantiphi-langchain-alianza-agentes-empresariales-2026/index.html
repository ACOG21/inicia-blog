Quantiphi and LangChain Partnered: Agent Engineering Is Now an Enterprise Discipline | inicIA

By inicIA · October 15, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 14, 2026, Quantiphi — an AI-native services company — and LangChain — the company behind the LangSmith platform — announced a strategic partnership to help enterprises adopt agent engineering at scale: from the first use case to company-wide rollout, with measurable value. The partnership combines LangChain's enterprise agent-engineering platform (LangSmith, LangGraph, and Deep Agents) with Quantiphi's forward-deployed engineering and AI transformation expertise across financial services, insurance, healthcare and life sciences, and telecom.

The partnership's three priorities

  • Governance and observability: observability, evaluation, and governance tooling for agentic transformation in the enterprise.
  • Embedded engineering: engineers deployed alongside client teams to scale workflows and systems on LangChain's platform.
  • Control without losing portability: agents are built inside the customer's secure environments — the company keeps its data, context, and IP — with flexibility across frontier and open-weight models, and across clouds.

Why it matters: from experiment to discipline

The quote that captures the moment comes from Siddharth Kotwal, Quantiphi's head of frontier AI GTM: succeeding with agentic AI in the enterprise requires domain knowledge, active collaboration with business experts, and curating the data flywheels that improve agents. Translated: scattered pilots are no longer enough. What separates a demo from a deployment is a named discipline — agent engineering — that includes continuous evaluation, observability, and governance from the first use case. The stated goal: move from fragmented experimentation to AI self-sufficiency without sacrificing portability or control.

What to copy even if you are neither company's customer

  1. Start with a use case with measurable value: the partnership promises "measurable value" from the first case. If your vendor cannot define the success metric before starting, do not start.
  2. Demand observability from day one: seeing what the agent did, with what data, and with what result is not a luxury — it is the requirement for running in production.
  3. Keep your data and context: the right architecture keeps data inside your environment and lets you switch models or clouds without rewriting everything.
  4. Engineers beside your team, not a PDF: the forward-deployed engineering model (technical people working shoulder to shoulder with your operation) is what turns pilots into systems.
