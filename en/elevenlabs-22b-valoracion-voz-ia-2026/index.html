ElevenLabs Is Worth $22 Billion: AI Voice Is the Hottest Enterprise Category | inicIA

By inicIA · October 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

On September 30, 2026, ElevenLabs confirmed the closing of a $300 million employee tender offer valuing it at $22 billion —double the $11 billion of its February 2026 Series D, when it raised $500 million. The tender, led by Wellington and T. Rowe Price with participation from Andreessen Horowitz, Lightspeed, EQT, Goldman Sachs, GIC and Ontario Teachers', among others, is not a new capital round: it is a secondary transaction giving employees liquidity. The number that matters for your business: its voice agents handle more than 15 million conversations per week —triple February's level— processing refunds, renewing insurance policies and booking appointments. Enterprises already generate 55% of its revenue. AI voice stopped being a demo: it is the enterprise AI category monetizing fastest.

The phenomenon in numbers

  • $22 billion: the tender valuation, 2x the $11B of February 2026.
  • $300 million: the tender is secondary —the money goes to employees selling shares, not to the company's coffers. It is the company's second secondary (the previous one: $100M at a $6.6B valuation in September 2025).
  • 15 million conversations per week: 3x since February. Real tasks: refunds, insurance renewals, scheduling.
  • 55% of revenue comes from enterprises; its models speak, listen and translate in more than 90 languages, used by 5.5 billion people.
  • Eleven v4 and v4 Turbo: its new text-to-speech models, launched alongside the announcement; and ElevenAgents, an orchestration engine that plugs into each enterprise's private databases and customer software stacks.
  • Founded in 2022 by CEO Mati Staniszewski and CTO Piotr Dabkowski, operating in New York and London: already among Europe's most valuable startups.

"We're already seeing rapid adoption of expressive voice agents by enterprises and governments, who are deploying them in service of consumers and citizens," said Staniszewski.

Why voice is the most profitable bet

Calls are a business's most measurable channel: minutes, resolutions, closed sales. A well-designed voice conversation resolves what twenty chat messages cannot, and answers at 8pm when nobody picks up. That institutional investors like Wellington and T. Rowe Price —who buy to hold through an IPO— put $300M into employee liquidity tells you where the smart money is: in the voice that sells, collects and schedules.

Frequently asked questions

Is a tender offer the same as a funding round?

No. In a round, the money goes into the company to operate. In a tender, investors buy existing shares from employees: it is liquidity for the team, not new capital for the company. The $22B valuation does reflect what the market believes the company is worth.

Does AI voice still sound robotic?

That was the 2023 objection. Current models (like Eleven v4) generate expressive voices indistinguishable in short service calls, and handle interruptions, pauses and multiple languages in the same conversation.

What does a voice agent do that a chatbot cannot?

It makes and takes calls: it resolves in minutes what a chat resolves in twenty messages, answers after hours, and converts better because voice builds more trust than text.

Is this only for large enterprises?

No: ElevenLabs reports 55% of its revenue already comes from businesses of all sizes. A small business with high volumes of repetitive calls is the perfect use case.
