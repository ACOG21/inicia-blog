Otter.ai Raises $320M from General Catalyst: Voice as the Enterprise Knowledge Layer | inicIA

By inicIA · October 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 20, 2026, Otter.ai announced $320 million in growth financing from General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund (CVF) to turn its meeting notetaker into the enterprise knowledge layer — where every conversation is captured, connected and turned into action (TechCrunch, PYMNTS, Business Wire). The funds go to enterprise growth, go-to-market strategy, international expansion and product innovation, focused on agentic workflows built on voice. The company's numbers: over 40 million users (25 million a year ago), teams at more than 86% of Fortune 500 companies, over 1 billion meetings transcribed and $1.5 billion in customer ROI from time saved. The company crossed $100 million in annual recurring revenue in 2025; its prior equity raised totals about $50 million (last round in 2021, per PitchBook).

The Detail That Matters Most: Capital Without Diluting Equity

  • Unusual structure: General Catalyst's CVF provides capital without diluting equity — its returns are tied to the results of marketing spend, not an ownership stake (TechCrunch).
  • The read: a top investor is willing to fund a voice company's growth without demanding equity. "Voice is one of the biggest untapped opportunities in enterprise AI," said Pranav Singhvi, partner and co-founder of CVF.
  • The stated ambition: "build enterprise agentic workflows" and make voice "the backbone of enterprise productivity" (Sam Liang, CEO of Otter.ai). The game has moved from transcribing meetings to AI acting on what was said.

The Lesson for Your Business: Your Meetings Are a Data Goldmine

  1. Voice is your company's most wasted asset: every week, decisions are made, promises to clients are given and objections surface in meetings nobody records. Otter is betting that whoever captures that knowledge wins.
  2. From transcript to action: the company is already testing a pre-brief that arrives before a call with context from past conversations, and Live Assist to query the assistant mid-meeting. The question: does your business turn what was said into tasks, follow-ups and proposals — or does it evaporate?
  3. The market is consolidating: competitors include Fireflies.ai, Granola, Read AI, and newcomers like Calendly, Wispr and Superhuman (which acquired Fathom). The honest caveat from the coverage: Otter disclosed no monthly active users or churn. Retention, not signups, decides who survives (TechCrunch, Techflier).
  4. Voice + agents = the next leap: the money is going to agentic workflows that use conversation data to move processes beyond transcription. If your business already records and transcribes, the next step is agents that execute what was agreed.
