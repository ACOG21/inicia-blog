Anthropic brought Claude to every level of government at a 40% discount: the price war reached the public counter

By inicIA · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 27, 2026, the U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) announced an expanded OneGov agreement with Anthropic: government buyers get a 40% discount on token-based usage of Claude models available to the government, including FedRAMP-authorized environments for sensitive unclassified work. There is no minimum purchase: agencies pay only for what they use. For the first time, the discount also reaches state, local, tribal and territorial governments, in addition to the federal government. Per Nextgov, the agreement runs 27 months, to January 31, 2029.

From a symbolic dollar to pay-per-use

  • What expires: the prior agreement gave federal agencies access to Claude for Enterprise and Claude for Government for $1 per user. That promotional offer expires on October 31, 2026.
  • What replaces it: a 40% discount on metered token usage, on Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government and the Claude API, bought directly now and through cloud providers in the coming months, per Anthropic.
  • How to buy: through GSA's Multiple Award Schedule at the OneGov tier, directly, through resellers, and on major cloud platforms. Federal eligibility covers the executive, legislative and judicial branches.
  • The savings estimate is the company's: Anthropic estimates the agreement will save the government about $1 billion. That figure is Anthropic's estimate, not an audited saving.

The context: the same move, at every lab

  • OpenAI made a parallel move: Nextgov reports OpenAI closed a 50% discount on token-based usage through its own OneGov agreement, in effect since early October for 27 months. Competition for the public customer is already fought on price per token.
  • The OneGov program in GSA figures: GSA says OneGov has generated more than $1.8 billion in savings since its inception, including about $1.6 billion through super-intelligence agreements.
  • Adoption reported by Anthropic: about 500 U.S. government organizations already use Claude, with weekly Claude for Government active users up about eightfold since February and usage across federal civilian agencies up about twentyfold in a year, per the company and without independent verification in the source.
  • The risk the discount does not cover: a low price does not settle permissions, logs, data retention or human review of decisions affecting citizens. The discount buys access, not governance.

What it means for your business

  • Price per token became the currency: when the government buys by discounted tokens with no minimum, the enterprise AI business model is clear: you pay for metered use, not a flat license. Your vendor contract should reflect that same logic.
  • The lesson of the promotional offer: the $1-per-agency price always had an expiry date. If your company lives on a promotional price, plan for the day after expiry before it arrives.
  • Before signing on price: measure your real consumption for a month, with your real use cases, and compare the discounted cost against the undiscounted cost. A 40% discount on consumption you do not control is still an open bill.

Frequently asked questions

What discount does Anthropic offer the government?

A 40% discount on token-based usage of Claude models available to the government, under the expanded OneGov agreement announced by GSA on October 27, 2026.

Who can buy at that discount?

Federal (executive, legislative and judicial), state, local, tribal and territorial governments, directly, through resellers, and on major cloud platforms.

What happens to the $1-per-agency offer?

It expires on October 31, 2026. It is replaced by the 40% discount on token-based usage, with no minimum purchase.

Is the $1 billion saving verified?

No. It is Anthropic's estimate, not a saving audited by GSA or by an independent third party.
