Google DeepMind introduced Genie 4: worlds you walk through and change by typing

By inicIA · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 27, 2026, Google DeepMind announced Genie 4, its newest world model: from text or an image it generates a scene you can walk through in real time with a keyboard, with native audio including music, ambient sound and character dialogue. While the scene runs, you can type a change, such as rain, a different camera angle or a new person, and the world adjusts. For now it is a research preview for invited testers, with access coming soon to Google AI Ultra subscribers through Project Genie and early-access partners of the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. A waitlist is open.

What changed from Genie 3

  • Native audio for the first time in the line: sound is generated together with the picture, not added afterwards. Characters converse and react to what happens in the scene in real time.
  • Directed by Gemini: Gemini Omni supplies appearance and movement, and Gemini 3.8 Flash acts as director: it splits a goal into steps, checks each frame and replans when the user intervenes, per The Verge's coverage.
  • Coherent scenes for longer: TechCrunch reports a scene now holds together for up to two minutes, double Genie 3's limit of about one minute.
  • Better physics and characters: Google describes better physics, collisions and aerodynamics, and characters that react more naturally. DeepMind itself notes limits: objects still drift over time, text inside the scene is not always legible, and long-term consistency remains unsolved.

The word 'first' deserves an asterisk

  • Google's claim: DeepMind presents Genie 4 as its first real-time world model that generates audio natively.
  • The nuance: specialist coverage notes that Runway released GWM Worlds 2 a couple of months earlier, also playable and also with audio. The word 'first' is, for now, Google's claim, not an established fact.
  • Watermark: Google says all generated video and audio will carry SynthID, its hidden watermark for AI-made content. For a company, that detail matters when material leaves the lab.
  • Uses Google proposes: film pre-visualization, game-idea prototyping, and training scenarios for robots and AI agents, with enterprise partners testing fast scenario generation.

What it means for your business

  • Prototype before building: the nearest business application is testing an idea in a walkable world before writing engine code, filming or building a set. A playable prototype shortens the conversation with a client or partner.
  • Train without breaking anything real: for robots and agents, a simulated world lets you test scenarios that in the real world would cost time, money or risk. That is the training path DeepMind highlights.
  • What to ask before buying: real useful session length, how fast the scene degrades, who owns the generated material, and how it is marked as AI-generated when published.

Frequently asked questions

What is Genie 4?

It is Google DeepMind's world model announced on October 27, 2026. It generates real-time interactive scenes, with native audio, that you can walk through and modify with text while they run.

Can I use Genie 4 already?

Not publicly yet. It is a research preview for invited testers, with access coming soon to Google AI Ultra subscribers through Project Genie and early-access partners. A waitlist is open.

How is it different from a video generator?

A video generator produces a clip to watch. A world model produces a place to walk through: you move, the camera responds, and you can change the scene while it runs.

Is Genie 4 the first world model with audio?

Google presents it that way for its line. Specialist coverage notes Runway released GWM Worlds 2 earlier, also playable and with audio, so the 'first' claim is best read with that nuance.
