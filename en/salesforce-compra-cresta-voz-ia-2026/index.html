Salesforce will buy Cresta for a reported ~$2.7B: voice becomes the center of customer service

By inicIA · November 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 2, 2026, Salesforce signed a definitive agreement to acquire Cresta, the voice customer-experience company founded in 2017 out of Stanford's AI Lab. Cresta describes itself as a voice-first operating system for customer experience: agents that resolve complex calls end to end, live coaching for human representatives during the call, and analysis of every conversation. Salesforce plans to join that conversation with the customer history in Service Cloud and its Agentforce agents. Salesforce did not disclose the price; The Information reports about $2.7 billion, a figure neither company has confirmed. Closing is expected in Salesforce's fiscal Q1 2028 (February to April 2027), subject to regulatory approvals and customary closing conditions.

What Cresta brings and why it matters

  • End-to-end voice agents: live conversations that handle interruptions and resolve complex calls, not just FAQ scripts. Salesforce cites Marriott, Comcast and Alaska Airlines among its enterprise customers.
  • Live coaching for humans: the same platform guides the human representative while talking, with real-time suggestions. Voice stops being only automation and becomes assistance to the team.
  • Conversation joined to history: by connecting the call to Service Cloud, resolution, retention and revenue stay tied to the conversation instead of living in separate systems.
  • A 2026 buying streak: Cresta joins Salesforce's 2026 customer-experience and marketing acquisitions, which per CMSWire include Contentful, Braze, Listen Labs and the company formerly known as Intercom (Fin).
  • Price with an asterisk: the $2.7 billion figure comes from a report, not from either company's statement. Cresta had raised more than $280 million per PitchBook and was valued at $1.6 billion in a 2022 round. Until Salesforce files deal documents or confirms terms, the price is cited as reported.

What to do with this news in your business

  • Demand voice with history, not voice alone: when evaluating a voice agent, ask that the call be logged against the customer history, with outcome and reason for human handoff.
  • Measure resolution and handoff, not minutes talked: the metric that matters is how many calls resolve fully, how many escalate and why, and how fast the first useful answer arrives.
  • Keep the human channel for the complex: Cresta's model pairs voice agents with live coaching for humans. Copy that combination: automate the repetitive and assist, not replace, the team on the delicate.
  • Plan migration calmly: the deal has not even closed, with closing expected February-April 2027. Nobody needs to switch platforms this week over an announcement; what pays is asking for the integration roadmap before signing long renewals.

Note: this article summarizes Salesforce's November 2, 2026 statement and coverage by CMSWire, SiliconANGLE, The Stack and The Information, picked up by ShiftDelete, AppStackInsider, TSN Media and ValueAddVC. The $2.7 billion price is reported, not disclosed by Salesforce or Cresta, and the deal remains subject to regulatory approvals.
