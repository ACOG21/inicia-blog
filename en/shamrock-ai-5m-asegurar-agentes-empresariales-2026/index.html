Shamrock AI Raised $5M: Who Tests Your Agents Before They Go to Production | inicIA

By inicIA · October 15, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 14, 2026, startup Shamrock AI raised $5 million in a seed round led by Endiya Partners, with participation from Capria Ventures — its first institutional round. Founded in 2026 by Sarat Addanki, Anuraag Nallapati, and Michael (Mick) Gallagher, headquartered in Shillong (India) with offices in Hyderabad and San Francisco, the company builds an agentic assurance platform: it helps large enterprises deploy AI agents reliably across critical business processes. Its centerpiece is an Enterprise Business Knowledge Graph connecting business processes, systems, data, and configurations, so agents understand how the organization operates before acting. It already serves multiple Fortune 500 companies, including a Fortune 100 AI hardware manufacturer and a Fortune 100 healthcare company.

Where it starts: ERP migrations

Shamrock's initial focus is agentic test lifecycle automation for large ERP transformations, including SAP S/4HANA migrations. The logic: continuously testing and validating business processes builds an increasingly accurate representation of how the enterprise operates — and that representation is the foundation for data observability and autonomous automation of critical processes later. The capital goes to product, team growth across India and the U.S. (70–80% of headcount in India), and Middle East expansion.

Why it matters: the exam before deployment

This round tells a bigger story: putting agents into production untested is now a market-recognized risk. The expensive failures prove it — the airline chatbot that invented a refund policy and the company had to pay, the courier bot that insulted its own customers. Shamrock's thesis: agents need exact business context (how your processes, systems, and data operate) and continuous testing before touching critical operations. Assurance is not a brake: it is what lets you accelerate with confidence.

The checklist before putting an agent into production

  1. A map of your processes: the agent must "know" how your business operates — processes, systems, data — before executing. If your vendor never asks for that map, worry.
  2. Continuous testing, not one demo: validate the agent's behavior repeatedly against your real processes, not once in a toy environment.
  3. A human approval gate on decisions that move money or sensitive data.
  4. Observability from day one: what it did, with what data, with what result — logged and auditable.
