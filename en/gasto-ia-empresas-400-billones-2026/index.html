Enterprise AI Spending Nears $400B, but Only 23% Scale Agents: the Eternal Pilot Trap | inicIA

By inicIA · October 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 12, 2026, the FII Institute published its report "Transforming Business: AI in the Enterprise" with a double data point every business owner should read twice: global enterprise spending on artificial intelligence nears $400 billion in 2026 (IDC estimate), but only 23% of organizations have managed to scale AI agents in at least one business function. Citing McKinsey's "State of AI": 88% of companies use AI in at least one function (up from 78% in 2024) and 62% are experimenting with agents — yet between experimenting and scaling lies an abyss. Your business does not compete on buying AI: it competes on putting it to work.

The full picture from the report

The FII Institute report (with contributions from Brookfield, JD.com and Visa) identifies three blockers:

  • Scale is the bottleneck, not the models: 62% experiment with agents, 23% scale them. Orchestration, permissions and governance are the obstacle, not model capability.
  • Security and trust lead the agenda: per Deloitte research cited in the report, data privacy and security is the top concern for 73% of executives; followed by legal compliance, intellectual property and regulation at 50%.
  • Value lives in redesigning work: the report points to workflow redesign, workforce development and responsible governance as the path to turn investment into measurable results.

Why almost nobody escapes the pilot

An AI pilot is cheap, visible and safe: a pretty demo nobody has to defend in a board meeting. Scaling it demands redesigning processes, giving the agent real permissions (and auditing them), training the team and accepting that 20% of the work changes hands. That is the trap: the pilot is funded by the innovation budget; scale is funded by the operations budget — and that is where accountability lives.

How to avoid the eternal pilot in your business

  1. Start with a process that moves money: prospect follow-up, qualification, scheduling. If the pilot touches neither revenue nor costs, scale will never get a budget.
  2. Measure from day one: define the KPI before turning the agent on (response time in minutes, appointments booked, qualified prospects). Without a metric, the pilot is eternal by definition.
  3. Buy governance, not just capability: agent permissions, audit logs and spending limits are what separate an experiment from an operation.
  4. Redesign the flow, not just the step: automating a broken step inside a broken process produces a faster error.
