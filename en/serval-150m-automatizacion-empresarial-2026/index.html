Serval raised $150M at $3B: agent automation is now sold as a replacement, not an experiment

By inicIA · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 29, 2026, Serval announced a $150 million Series C led by Sequoia, at a $3 billion valuation, bringing its total raised to $277 million. It is the company's third round in 12 months, after a $47 million Series A in October 2025 and a $75 million Series B in December 2025, also led by Sequoia, at $1 billion. The company plans to hire more than 500 people in 12 months, including more than 100 automation engineers for a new services arm called ProServal.

What Serval sells, specifically

  • Catalyst, its automation agent: a team describes a workflow in natural language and the agent produces, tests and publishes the full automation, running under strict enterprise controls.
  • Guardrails by design: per the company, automations include permission checks and administrator-supervised approvals, because letting the model decide alone remains a weak security policy.
  • Beyond IT: the capital will take Catalyst into HR, finance, security and legal in addition to IT, and open offices in New York, London and Austin, per launch coverage.
  • The stated ambition: Serval positions itself as an alternative to established platforms such as ServiceNow, which reported $13.8 billion in 2025 revenue per coverage. Sequoia backed ServiceNow in 2009 and now funds its would-be replacement.

What to read carefully before celebrating the valuation

  • Growth is a company claim: Serval says its annual recurring revenue doubled every quarter since the Series B and that two Fortune 20 companies became customers in the prior six months. It did not disclose the recurring-revenue figure, so that claim cannot be verified from outside.
  • The services arm is the real signal: hiring more than 100 automation engineers to deploy alongside customers says something the valuation does not: agent automation still needs implementation work, not just software.
  • The question for your business: does your workflow have permissions, approvals and a record of who authorized what? If the answer is no, even the cheapest agent on the market is not ready for that workflow yet.
