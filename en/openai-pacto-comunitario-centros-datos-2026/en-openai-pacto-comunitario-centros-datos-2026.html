OpenAI promises to pay its data centers' power bills and stop using NDAs: the pact that answers the ballot box

By inicIA · November 6, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 5, 2026, OpenAI published its National Community Compact and a Compute Campus Policy Blueprint: a set of commitments on how it will build its compute campuses. The company promises to pay the full cost of electricity and infrastructure its campuses need, stop asking public officials to sign non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) when negotiating sites, and publish water and energy use per site, alongside cutting consumption at peak hours and returning electricity to the grid. The announcement comes two days after the November 3 elections, in which, per the reports, 40 of 41 local measures in 10 states went in favor of limiting data centers or against proposals that encouraged them. The compact applies only to future campuses OpenAI owns and operates.

The numbers that matter

  • 40 of 41 local measures: in the November 3 elections, voters decided against data-center development in 40 of 41 local measures across 10 states, per Ballotpedia and CNBC reporting cited in the coverage.
  • Up to 1,000 megawatts of peak reduction: OpenAI says a contract with Georgia Power for its planned Effingham County site will let it cut its power supply by up to 1,000 megawatts when demand is high.
  • Full cost of electricity and infrastructure: the company will pay for the electricity its owned campuses use and the infrastructure needed to supply it, with financial assurances before major spending, so other users do not carry the cost if a project is delayed, scaled back or cancelled.
  • NDAs out, with narrow exceptions: OpenAI will stop asking public officials for non-disclosure agreements, except in narrow cases such as security information, trade secrets or competitively sensitive data.
  • One local compact per campus: each future campus will have its own compact with the community, with local hiring goals, a water plan per site with preference for closed-loop systems, and independent noise studies before operations and every year after.

What it means for your business

  • The neighborhood permit is already a line in the AI budget: AI infrastructure is also decided at local ballot boxes. If your company plans its own compute capacity or leases near a campus, the political and community cost of the project is part of the total cost, not a surprise.
  • Electricity is the friction point: the compact's central commitment is that households do not pay for data centers' electricity. For any business with energy-intensive consumption, the argument that convinces a community is paying the full cost and proving it with per-site data.
  • The compact's scope is the real limit: the commitments cover only campuses OpenAI owns and operates. Sites built and run by partners, landlords or cloud providers, where OpenAI is only a tenant, fall outside what was announced. Before citing the compact as a standard, check which sites it covers.
  • The company asks for the rules to become law: the policy blueprint that accompanies the compact asks federal, state and local lawmakers to turn similar commitments into binding requirements. Today's self-regulation is tomorrow's regulation, and it pays to design operations to that standard now.

Note: this article summarizes OpenAI's November 5, 2026 announcement and coverage from The Hindu BusinessLine, AI Daily News, Tech Insider and Synapse. The local measure results and the Georgia Power contract details are reported. The commitments are voluntary and apply to future campuses OpenAI owns and operates; the reports do not establish how they would extend to partner facilities.
