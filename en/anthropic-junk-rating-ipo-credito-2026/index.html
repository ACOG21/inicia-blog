Blackstone expects Anthropic to get a junk rating after its IPO: what the cost of money means for AI

By inicIA · November 1, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 30, 2026, the Financial Times reported, via TechCrunch coverage, that Blackstone told investors it expects Anthropic to receive a high speculative-grade rating, in the double-B range, after its IPO. That expectation replaces Blackstone's earlier guidance, which pointed to investment grade before 2027. The central caveat: this is an investor's expectation, not a published rating. Moody's, S&P and Fitch had not published an Anthropic rating as of that date, and both Anthropic and Blackstone declined to comment, per TechCrunch.

What is known and what is still a forecast

  • The IPO is underway: Anthropic filed its S-1 prospectus on October 26, 2026 to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. The share count, price range and offering date have not been set.
  • The $15 billion credit line: per the prospectus, the line stays secured by substantially all of the company's assets until it receives investment-grade ratings from at least two of the big three agencies.
  • The debt already disclosed: the prospectus reports $6.2 billion in debt as of June 30, 2026, all in convertible notes held by Amazon.
  • Project North Star, the chip financing: Blackstone is the anchor investor in a $63 billion financing to lease about 1.5 GW of Google chips. Per the report, more than $42 billion in senior loans carry a Broadcom guarantee, about $18 billion in junior debt is backed by Anthropic's lease payments, and Blackstone funds put about $9 billion into the junior debt at yields above 10%.
  • The partial ratings that were reported: Venture Magazine coverage notes that KBRA already rated junior tranches BB+ with a positive outlook, and Morningstar DBRS assigned BB. Those are ratings of specific tranches, not the corporate rating of the big three agencies.

What it means for your business

  • Intelligence has a financing cost: when your model provider pays more to borrow, that cost eventually pressures prices, contracts and investment pace. A speculative-grade provider keeps operating, with money that costs more.
  • Ask about commitments, not only about models: before building processes on a provider, review what long-term compute commitments it assumed and how it finances them. Product announcements speak of capability; prospectuses and credit reports speak of what it costs to sustain it.
  • Diversify provider risk: if a single model supports a critical process in your business, a change in its cost or roadmap leaves you exposed. Keeping a second viable provider is insurance, not a luxury.
  • Read the date on every figure: the $965 billion private valuation from the last round describes what investors paid for growth. A credit rating describes what lenders charge for risk. They are two different judgments about the same company, and the second one sets the cost of money.

Frequently asked questions

Has Anthropic already received a junk rating?

No. Blackstone told investors it expects a high double-B rating after the IPO. Moody's, S&P and Fitch had not published a company rating as of October 30, 2026.

What is Project North Star?

A $63 billion financing, with Blackstone as anchor investor, to lease about 1.5 GW of Google chips, with senior tranches guaranteed by Broadcom and junior debt backed by lease payments.

Why does a rating matter to an AI buyer?

Because a speculative-grade rating raises the provider's borrowing cost and shrinks the pool of institutional buyers for its debt, which can pressure prices and investment pace.

Does the Anthropic IPO already have a date and price?

No. The S-1 was filed on October 26, 2026 under the ticker ANT, and the share count, price range and offering date have not been set.
