Claude now joins your Google Meet calls: it takes notes, answers and builds documents live

By inicIA · November 15, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

Anthropic started rolling out Claude Meetings in beta for Team and Enterprise plans, with Google Meet as the first video platform. Claude joins the call as a visible participant, follows the conversation, answers team questions and builds documents and whiteboards while the meeting continues. At the end, Claude delivers structured notes and action items. Anthropic announced the feature on November 10 as coming soon, and its launch page is dated November 12, 2026. Notes and transcripts are free during the beta on Team and Enterprise plans, and documents, whiteboards and other artifacts created in the call are free through February 10, 2027.

What Claude does inside the call

  • It joins as a visible participant: Claude appears in the call with its own panel. It is invited from Claude Meetings, or by adding claude@meetings.claude.ai from any active Google Meet call.
  • It takes notes and action items in real time: Claude follows the discussion, writes the notes and the follow-ups, and produces a structured notes file at the end of the meeting.
  • It answers team questions in the chat: participants ask during the call and Claude answers with the context of the conversation and of connected services.
  • It builds documents and whiteboards live: Claude creates documents, whiteboards and slides while the meeting continues, without waiting for the post-meeting summary.
  • It records no audio: Anthropic says Claude records no audio from the call. Notes and transcripts are the record, and they stay available to revisit later.

The limits to know before turning it on

  • Google Meet only for now: Anthropic says other video platforms come later, with no names or dates published. If your company's standard is Zoom or Microsoft Teams, the feature waits.
  • Team and Enterprise only: the beta reaches Team and Enterprise plans. On Team, Meetings turns on by default; on Enterprise, it stays off by default and the administrator turns it on.
  • External guests change the behavior: by default, Claude stays out of calls with guests from outside the organization and leaves if an outsider joins partway through the call.
  • Some configurations stay out of the beta: organizations with zero data retention, a HIPAA-ready configuration or customer-managed encryption keys stay out of the beta for now.
  • The calendar is part of the setup: the Google Calendar connection is enabled first by the organization's owner. The call invitation runs through Meetings in Claude, on the web or in the desktop app.

What to do in your business this week

  • Try Claude Meetings in an internal meeting: a team meeting without external guests is the place to measure whether the notes and action items come out with the quality your team expects. The internal test costs one meeting; the first test on a client call costs reputation.
  • Define the external-guest rule before turning on auto-join: the auto-join setting lives under Settings, Capabilities, Meetings. Decide in writing which meetings Claude joins and which it stays out of, and share the rule with the team.
  • Review who receives the notes: notes and transcripts stay in Claude to revisit. Define who reads them, where they are stored and how long they are kept, with the same rule you use for your meeting minutes.
  • Measure the real saving in the first week: compare the time your team spent writing minutes and passing on action items against the time it spends reviewing Claude's notes. The saving is measured in minutes per meeting, and the decision to keep it rests on that number.
