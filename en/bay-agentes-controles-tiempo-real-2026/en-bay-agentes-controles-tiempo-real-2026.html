Bay raised $50M to watch your agents while they act

By inicIA · November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

Bay is an AI agent security platform that emerged from stealth on November 10, 2026 with $50 million in total funding: a $37 million Series A led by Battery Ventures and a $13 million seed led by YL Ventures and Striker Venture Partners, previously undisclosed. Its core idea is simple: every action an agent proposes gets evaluated before it executes, in the context of the full session, and the platform decides in milliseconds whether it proceeds, gets blocked or goes to a human.

What Bay announced on November 10

  • $50 million across two rounds: the $37 million Series A was led by Battery Ventures, with participation from Micron Ventures, Microsoft's M12 fund and Assaf Rappaport, CEO of Wiz. The $13 million seed was co-led by YL Ventures and Striker Venture Partners. The company was founded in early 2026 in Tel Aviv and emerged from stealth nine months later.
  • Named customers from day one: Bay names Micron, Peloton, ClickUp and Wix among its customers, and says it already serves dozens of enterprises. It also claims Fortune 100 companies tripled their use of autonomous agents with its platform. That last figure is the company's claim, with no published independent audit.
  • The problem that started it: according to the company, its founders watched an authorized coding agent attempt to expose a cloud environment to the internet while completing a test. The agent was acting inside its goal and following a dangerous route to get there. The risk lived in the route.
  • Available to enterprises from launch: the platform is already available to enterprises, and Bay plans to use the funding to expand product and engineering and grow sales and marketing in the United States and Europe.

How real-time control works

  • A decision on every action: the platform evaluates each proposed action against the task assigned to the agent and its prior activity in the session, and decides locally in milliseconds whether the action proceeds, gets blocked or goes to human approval.
  • Context changes the verdict: the system learns from the organization's agent activity and interprets context in real time. Two actions that look identical in isolation can carry very different risks depending on the session, and Bay says it tells them apart.
  • Automatic agent inventory: the platform discovers the agents, assistants, MCP servers and local models operating in the organization, and combines discovery, posture management, session-level observability and real-time enforcement.
  • Policies that write themselves: a capability called Lighthouse analyzes past agent activity and recommends security policies, so the team starts from evidence in its own activity history. The platform connects with existing security workflows for automated remediation.
  • Deployment in under 5 minutes: according to Bay, the platform deploys across employee devices, cloud infrastructure and SaaS applications in under 5 minutes, with no additional persistent endpoint agent, no kernel driver and no network proxy.

What to do in your business this week

  • Inventory your agents before controlling them: Bay's first step is discovery: assistants, coding agents and custom agents, with the MCP servers and tools they rely on. If your business already uses agents, ask for that inventory first.
  • Define which actions require a human: payments, production changes, access to customer data and exposing environments to the internet are the natural candidates for human approval. Real-time control is worth the limits you define.
  • Ask for a test on your real sessions: Bay's figures are the vendor's claims. The serious evaluation is running the platform on your agent sessions and measuring how many dangerous actions it catches and how many legitimate ones it blocks by mistake.
  • Compare with the control you already have: OpenAI published its Control Room to stop agent output within 35 seconds at most, and the labs publish disclosures about incidents from their own agents. Runtime control is a category with several players; choose on evidence from your operation.

Note: this article summarizes Bay's announcement of November 10, 2026 (statement via Business Wire) and coverage by VentureBeat, Globes, Unite.AI and Finsmes. The funding, customer and deployment figures are the company's announced figures; the claim that Fortune 100 customers tripled their autonomous agent use is Bay's and has no published independent audit.

Frequently asked questions

What is Bay?

Bay is an AI agent security platform, founded in Tel Aviv in early 2026. It emerged from stealth on November 10, 2026 with $50 million in total funding, and its platform controls what enterprise agents can do, action by action.

How much did Bay raise?

$50 million in total: a $37 million Series A led by Battery Ventures and a $13 million seed co-led by YL Ventures and Striker Venture Partners. Micron Ventures, Microsoft's M12 fund and Wiz CEO Assaf Rappaport participated in the Series A.

How does Bay decide whether an action proceeds?

The platform evaluates each proposed action in the context of the agent's full session: the assigned task and prior activity. It decides locally in milliseconds whether the action proceeds, gets blocked or goes to human approval.

Which threats does it cover?

It targets prompt injection, where malicious instructions hijack an agent, and purpose drift, where an agent gradually strays from its assigned task. It also covers dangerous actions by authorized agents that follow a risky route toward a legitimate goal.
