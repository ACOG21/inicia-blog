Case 1: Orange Spain — 1,000 agents in an "AI Factory"

On October 8, 2026, Orange Spain — Spain's leading telecommunications operator — and Google Cloud announced an expanded partnership to industrialize its AI operations with Gemini Enterprise. The number: more than 1,000 custom AI agents deployed across human resources, IT, sales, and customer service. The use cases are operational and concrete: incident triage, network root-cause analysis, corporate knowledge access in natural language, and routine request management. The platform was built on a data foundation developed with Google Cloud since 2018 — a BigQuery data warehouse and a Cloud Storage data lake — with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounding agent responses in verified corporate data. The adoption result: nearly 100% license activity in 30 days, with non-technical employees building their own agents through a low-code framework, with no IT bottlenecks.

Case 2: Salesforce Agentforce — $800M annually and +169%

Salesforce turned its agents into a business line. According to its fiscal 2026 reports, Agentforce generates about $800 million in annual recurring revenue, growing 169% year over year. The scale: 29,000 Agentforce deals closed (December 2025) and over one million agent conversations logged. The interesting data point for your business: the platform lets companies create "co-worker agents" with their own email accounts and calendars and controlled access to shared resources. The lesson: agents stopped being an IT experiment and became a product that companies buy, measure, and renew.

Case 3: insurance agencies — 3 in 4 grow as AI triples

The 2026 Agency Universe Study (published by Future One, a collaboration of the Big "I" association with independent agency companies) found that three in four independent insurance agencies in the US reported revenue increases between 2024 and 2025, while the share reporting declines fell from 12% to 8% — all while AI adoption tripled in the sector. The study estimates about 37,000 independent agencies operating in the US, with average staff size growing from 8.2 to 9.9 employees. Revenue growth concentrated in both major lines: ~70% reported personal-lines increases and ~two-thirds commercial-lines increases. Correlation is not causation, but the sector that adopted AI fastest is the one that grew most.

The common pattern (what you can copy)

  1. Data first, agents second. Orange spent 8 years tidying its data foundation before the 1,000 agents. AI on messy data multiplies the mess.
  2. Agents on real workflows, not demos. Incident triage, document review, sales follow-up: all three cases applied AI where the work already existed.
  3. Employees build. Orange's low-code framework distributed agent creation across the organization. The 100% adoption in 30 days did not come from a mandate: it came from handing people the tools.
