Google Opened Gemini 4 Argon With Launch Pricing: $2 Per Million Tokens Until Nov 30 | inicIA

By inicIA · October 14, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 13, 2026, Google made Gemini 4 Argon globally available, its frontier reasoning model for deep work on complex tasks: coding, finance, legal, cybersecurity, and science. It reaches developers and enterprises via the API (AI Studio and the Gemini platform), AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, and Ultra subscribers in Antigravity. Launch pricing is aggressive: $2 per million input tokens and $10 output (95% off for cached input), valid through November 30, 2026; afterward it rises to $4/$20. Google says Argon ranks #1 on the Vals Index, which measures performance across critical economic sectors like finance, coding, legal, and tax.

The launch facts

  • Date: October 13, 2026 (the original announcement was September 30, with access limited to approved cybersecurity defenders).
  • Access routes: paid API in AI Studio and the Gemini platform (model gemini-4-argon); Gemini app and AI Mode in Search for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers; Antigravity for Ultra only.
  • Pricing: $2 input / $10 output per million tokens, cached input 95% off → valid through Nov 30, 2026; standard pricing afterward: $4/$20.
  • Capacity: unified 1,048,576-token context window; accepts text, images, video, audio, and PDF.
  • Google's internal proof: more than 100,000 employees use it; over four weeks, engineering teams increased agentic code submissions by 35% and reduced post-submission rollbacks by 30% (per launch coverage).

The price war intensifies

At $2/$10, Argon undercuts Claude Opus 5.5 ($4/$20) and GPT-6 Astra ($10/$50) during the introductory window. Meta answered the day before by matching Opus 5.5's price with Muse Wonder. For AI buyers, this is a temporary golden age: frontier model cost falls while capability rises. The obvious strategy: test and build during the discount window, with a cost plan for the standard price starting December 1.

What to do this week

  1. Measure cost per task, not per token: the cheapest model per token can cost more if it needs more calls to finish the job.
  2. Use the Nov 30 window: evaluate Argon on your real use cases now, with your own numbers, before the price doubles.
  3. Compare in your workflow: run the same process on two models and measure time, quality, and total cost before deciding.
