Microsoft and IBM sell 6,000 pre-built agents: catalog AI has arrived at the enterprise

By inicIA · November 17, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 10, 2026, IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise, an agentic platform to build, run and govern AI agents in industry-specific processes. IBM Consulting contributes more than 6,000 pre-built AI agents and assets, built on its IBM Consulting Advantage platform, and Microsoft contributes the AI, data, cloud and security foundation. The first industry frameworks are financial services, insurance and retail. No pricing was published.

What each partner brings to the table

  • IBM Consulting: the catalog: more than 6,000 pre-built agents and assets, with the experience of having built and run them for clients, and forward-deployed engineers for qualifying engagements. The sale is deployment experience, not just software.
  • Microsoft: the technology foundation: Microsoft Foundry, GitHub Copilot and Copilot Studio to coordinate agents, and Fabric, Microsoft 365, Entra, Purview, Defender, Sentinel and Azure for data, identity, governance, security and cloud.
  • Industries first: financial services, insurance and retail. Manufacturing, energy, public sector and mobility are named as next. The platform targets regulated industries first, where agent governance is the requirement.
  • The named customer is exploring, not deploying: IBM named SEI, a financial technology and asset management firm that manages, advises or administers about $2 trillion as of September 30, 2026, as a customer exploring the technology with IBM Consulting and Microsoft. Exploring is not deploying.

What the 6,000 number means (and what it does not mean)

  • They are agents and assets, not 6,000 ready bots: IBM describes a catalog of agents and assets: the number includes agents alongside workflow components, templates and reusable pieces. Reading it as 6,000 autonomous workers ready to switch on reads more than the announcement says.
  • The shift is from building to buying the operation: the new question for the buyer is whether it can put a governed agent operation into production and measure what it returns, not whether it can assemble an agent. The platform sells the operating layer: governance, observability and compliance.
  • Pricing is not in the announcement: no vendor in this wave published pricing, client counts or revenue from these platforms. Access runs through IBM Consulting engagements or self-managed options. Ask for the price in writing before comparing.
  • Governance is half the product: the announcements describe controls, observability and compliance support as central. In regulated industries, an agent that cannot be audited cannot be deployed.

What to do in your business this week

  • If you evaluate a catalog agent, ask for the list by process: which agent covers which of your processes, which systems it connects to, and what happens when the process changes. A large catalog does not guarantee your process is inside.
  • Ask who governs the agent: who approves its actions, where the record stays, how a permission is revoked, and who answers when it makes a mistake. If the vendor cannot answer that, the agent is not ready for your company.
  • Compare buying against building with your numbers: license and deployment cost, time to production, and operating cost. The pre-built agent wins when the process is standard; the built one wins when the process is your advantage.
  • Demand a pilot with measurement: one process, a starting number and an ending number. A platform that will not be measured in production sells a promise, not an operation.

Note: this article summarizes the joint IBM and Microsoft announcement of November 10, 2026 and coverage by PYMNTS, Dataconomy, Mahlum Innovations and ByteHerald. The 6,000 figure counts agents and assets according to the announcement; no pricing was published and SEI is a customer in the exploration phase.
