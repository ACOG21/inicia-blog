30 AI Terms Every Business Owner Should Know (Glossary)

By inicIA · September 26, 2026 · 8 min read

Direct answer

This is the artificial intelligence glossary every business owner needs: 30 AI terms explained in plain English, in alphabetical order. Mastering this vocabulary lets you evaluate vendors, understand proposals, and decide on AI with your own judgment.

Bookmark it as a reference: when a vendor mentions RAG, fine-tuning, or context window in a quote, you will know what it means and why it matters for your operation.

The glossary: 30 AI terms in alphabetical order

1. AI agent

A system that receives a goal and works alone to achieve it, in a cycle of perceiving, reasoning, acting, and learning. It queries databases, takes actions in your tools —scheduling, updating records, sending emails— and completes tasks with minimal supervision. Read what an AI agent is.

2. AI copilot

An assistant built into a tool you already use —email, spreadsheet, CRM— that suggests, drafts, and completes tasks while you work. It lives inside your workflow, so it speeds up what you already do without changing your habits.

3. API

An interface that lets two systems communicate. Through an API, your website, CRM, or store uses an AI model without building one from scratch: they send a request and receive the answer ready to display or process.

4. Artificial intelligence

The field of computing that creates systems able to understand language, recognize images, make decisions, and learn from data. In business it serves customers, automates processes, and analyzes information at scale. Start here: what artificial intelligence means for businesses.

5. Automation

Technology that runs repetitive tasks without human intervention: emails, records, appointments, reports. AI-powered automation goes further: it understands natural language and decides in unscripted situations. More detail in what AI automation is.

6. Bias

A model's tendency to give skewed answers because its training data contained those patterns. It matters when screening candidates or granting credit: a good AI project measures bias and corrects it.

7. Chatbot

A program that converses following prepared answers or rules. It handles repetitive questions 24/7 —hours, prices, policies— at low cost per conversation. Its scope is conversational: it informs efficiently and understands how your customers speak better every day.

8. Computer vision

The branch of AI that interprets images and video: recognizing products, reading license plates, detecting production defects, counting people. It automates inspections that used to require constant human supervision.

9. Context window

The amount of text, measured in tokens, that a model considers in one interaction: instructions, documents, history. A large window analyzes long contracts without losing track.

10. Embeddings

Numerical representations that capture the meaning of words or documents. Thanks to them, AI finds information by meaning, not just exact words: searching "invoice problem" also finds "payment dispute."

11. Fine-tuning

Additional training of an existing model with your company's data: scripts, policies, tone of voice. The result responds like a trained member of your team, with your vocabulary and criteria.

12. Generative AI

The branch of AI that creates new content: text, images, audio, code. It learns patterns from millions of examples and produces original output from an instruction. The technology behind ChatGPT and image generators.

13. Hallucination

A convincing but made-up or incorrect answer. It happens when the model fills information gaps with its best guess instead of admitting it lacks the data. It is controlled by connecting it to verified sources with RAG and keeping human oversight on critical topics.

14. Integration

The connection between AI and your tools —CRM, calendar, email, store— so they work as one system. It turns an AI that chats into an AI that produces: it books real appointments and updates records.

15. Latency

The time a system takes to respond after receiving a request. In conversational AI, low latency delivers near-instant answers, key to making a voice agent or chat feel natural.

16. LLM (large language model)

A model trained on enormous volumes of text that understands and generates human language with great fluency. The engine behind ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini: the foundation of assistants, agents, and copilots.

17. Machine learning

The branch of AI where systems learn patterns from data instead of following hand-programmed rules. Every example makes them more accurate: this is how recommendations, fraud detection, and sales forecasts work.

18. Model

The trained "brain" of an AI system: it receives an input and produces an output. There are language, image, and voice models. Choosing the right one defines the quality, speed, and cost of your solution.

19. Neural network

A mathematical structure inspired by the brain: layers of interconnected nodes that learn patterns from examples. The base architecture of almost all modern AI, from voice recognition to language models.

20. NLP (natural language processing)

Technology that lets computers understand human language: interpreting a complaint, extracting data from an email, summarizing a call. It makes talking to a system feel like talking to a person.

21. Orchestration

Coordinating several AI agents or tools that collaborate on a complex process, each with its role: one serves, another checks inventory, another books. It divides the work and unites the results into a smooth experience.

22. Parameters

The internal values a model adjusts during training; in practice, its "size." More parameters usually mean stronger reasoning, at higher cost and lower speed. The ideal model balances response quality with budget.

23. Prompt

The instruction you give an AI model: a question, a command, a text with context. Clear instructions with context produce useful, actionable answers.

24. Prompt engineering

The technique of writing clear instructions for better answers: giving context, defining a role, asking for a format, showing examples. A good prompt is the difference between a generic answer and a ready-to-use one.

25. RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)

A technique that connects the model to your documents before answering: it searches your knowledge base for the relevant information and drafts based on it. The assistant answers with your company's real data, not guesses.

26. Temperature

A setting that controls how creative or predictable answers are. Low temperature: consistent answers, ideal for customer support. High: more variety, useful for brainstorming and creative copy.

27. Token

The smallest unit of text a model processes: roughly a word or part of one. AI APIs are billed per token, so understanding them lets you estimate costs and compare vendor proposals.

28. Training data

The information used to teach an AI model: texts, images, records. Its quality and diversity define what the model knows and how well it responds. A system trained on your company's data understands your business better than a generic one.

29. Webhook

An automatic message one application sends to another when an event happens: a payment received, a form completed. The signal that sets your AI automation in motion at the exact moment.

30. Zero-shot and few-shot

Two ways to ask for something new without training the model: zero-shot means asking with no examples ("classify this email"); few-shot means showing two or three examples first. With few-shot, the AI mirrors your format and criteria with remarkable fidelity.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I start if I know nothing about AI?

Start with the five terms that explain everything else: artificial intelligence, generative AI, prompt, chatbot, and automation. This glossary is in alphabetical order so you can check it whenever you hear a new term in a meeting or proposal.

Which terms should I know before hiring an AI project?

Master these six before signing: AI agent, RAG, integration, fine-tuning, hallucination, and token. With them you judge whether the system connects to your real data, how it controls errors, and how the cost is calculated.

What is the difference between automation and artificial intelligence?

Automation runs repetitive tasks with defined rules; artificial intelligence understands language, learns from data, and decides in new situations. AI automation combines both. Here is the full explanation of what AI automation is.

Will AI replace my employees?

AI automates the repetitive —answering FAQs, logging data, scheduling, follow-ups— and frees your team to sell, negotiate, and build relationships. The businesses with the best results use it to amplify their people.
