Google launched Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash: 15-second video, conversational editing and 30% lower pricing

By inicIA · November 7, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 5, 2026, Google made Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash generally available, its AI video creation and editing model. The headline change: it generates and edits native clips of up to 15 seconds, up from 10 seconds on Omni 1.1, with length selectable in one-second steps from 3 to 15. It also adds multi-turn conversational editing (fix the same clip with a follow-up message instead of starting over), stronger reference following to keep faces and products consistent, and a price of $0.07 per second of 720p video, 30% below Omni 1.1. It is available on the Gemini API, Google Cloud, Gemini for business and across Google products (Flow, Vids and the Gemini app).

The numbers that matter

  • 15 seconds, up from 10: maximum generation and editing length rises 50%. Length is selectable from 3 to 15 seconds in one-second steps; first-and-last-frame workflows are limited to 3 to 10 seconds.
  • $0.07 per second at 720p: the published price is 30% below Omni 1.1. Formats cover 16:9 and 9:16 at 360p, 720p, 1080p and 4K, per the model documentation.
  • Generally available for the first time: Omni 1.2 Flash is the first Gemini Omni version with general availability, backed by Google Cloud enterprise infrastructure. The API lists no free tier.
  • SynthID watermark: generated videos carry Google's imperceptible SynthID watermark, which identifies the content as AI-generated.
  • December 3, 2026: the Omni 1.1 alias will automatically route to 1.2 on that date, at 1.2 rates. Google sets the 1.2 support retirement horizon at November 5, 2027 or later.

What changes for a business that makes video

  • Conversational editing is the real workflow change: asking for “make the sky warmer and extend the final shot” on the same clip, instead of regenerating from scratch, turns AI video into a revision tool, not a prompt lottery.
  • 15 seconds covers the full short format: a 15-second clip is already a short ad, a product demo or a reel segment without cuts. Continuity, audio, rights and factual accuracy still need human review.
  • The per-second price falls, and so does the cost per finished piece: at $0.07 per second, a 15-second 720p clip costs about $1.05 via the API. The real cost of a finished piece is the iterations, and conversational editing cuts exactly that cost.
  • Migration has a date: if your workflow depends on Omni 1.1, the switch to 1.2 arrives on its own on December 3. In Flow, Veo 3.1 Fast, Veo 3.1 Quality and Omni 1.1 retire on December 5, 2026. Check your templates before then.

Note: this article summarizes Google's November 5, 2026 announcement on its official blog and Google Cloud documentation, verified against Kingy, Chrome Geek, ReaderSpice and 7minAI. Prices quoted are Google's published Gemini API prices; limits and costs in Flow, Vids and the Gemini app vary by plan and Google warns they can change.

Frequently asked questions

What is Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash?

Google's AI video creation and editing model, made generally available on November 5, 2026. It generates and edits 3-to-15-second clips with sound, with multi-turn conversational editing.

How much does it cost?

Google publishes $0.07 per second of 720p video on the Gemini API, 30% below Omni 1.1. A 15-second 720p clip costs about $1.05, plus input and text charges.

Where can it be used?

On the Gemini API (paid tier), Google Cloud, Gemini for business, Google Flow, Google Vids and the Gemini app. Availability and limits vary by product and plan.

What happens to Omni 1.1?

The Omni 1.1 alias automatically routes to 1.2 from December 3, 2026. In Flow, Veo 3.1 Fast, Veo 3.1 Quality and Omni 1.1 retire on December 5, 2026.
