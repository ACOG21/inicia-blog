How to Choose an AI Vendor: 7 Questions Before You Sign

By inicIA · October 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Direct answer

The right vendor is chosen by your use case, not by their demo. Before signing, ask these 7 questions: 1) which exact process will it automate, and how is it measured; 2) does it integrate two-way with my systems; 3) what happens to my data; 4) do success criteria go in writing before we start; 5) is pricing transparent; 6) who walks with me to production; 7) what support exists afterward. Two numbers explain why this matters: only 31% of marketing organizations have managed AI governance (Alexander Group, 2026), and among pilots that do reach production, 41% show negative ROI at 12 months for not having defined success criteria (Forrester/Anaconda).

The 7 questions, one by one

  • 1. Which exact process, and how is it measured? "Automating support" is not a process: "answering 100% of calls and booking into the calendar" is. Ask for the flow in writing.
  • 2. Does it integrate two-way with my systems? What the AI books, qualifies, or logs has to appear in your real CRM or calendar, not in a parallel dashboard. Without two-way integration, you are buying a toy.
  • 3. What happens to my data? Where it is stored, who sees it, whether it trains models, and how it gets deleted. If the vendor hesitates answering, that is the answer.
  • 4. Do success criteria go in writing? Which number the project has to move, and in how many months. Without this, 41% of graduated projects end in negative ROI at 12 months.
  • 5. Is pricing transparent? All-in per-minute or per-month pricing, build cost separated and in writing, and what happens if you want to leave. Reference pricing is in how much an AI voice agent costs in 2026.
  • 6. Who walks with me to production? Between 86% and 88% of pilots never graduate (Forrester/Anaconda). Ask how many of their projects are in production today with real clients, not in demo.
  • 7. What support exists after launch? Who adjusts the script when your prices change, how fast a fix ships, and what the monthly fee includes.

Red flags: when to walk away

  • Promises to automate everything without having seen your systems.
  • The demo uses fake data and never asks to test with yours.
  • Pricing appears only after three meetings.
  • Cannot introduce you to two real clients in production.
  • "The AI learns on its own, it needs no supervision": it needs human review, always.

The selection process in 3 steps

Step 1: ask for a trial with your real data, not the generic demo. Step 2: talk to two of the vendor's clients and ask what broke and how they fixed it. Step 3: start with a bounded pilot: one process, written success criteria, and an evaluation date. What works gets scaled; what does not gets cancelled cheaply.

Should I pick the cheapest vendor?

Pick the most transparent one. A low price without two-way integration or production support ends up costing more: the project never works and you pay twice. Compare total price and real projects in production, not just the quote.

What should the contract include?

The exact process to automate, success criteria with numbers and a date, itemized pricing (build vs. monthly), who owns your data, support response times, and exit terms. If any of this is missing, ask for it in writing before signing.

How long should a pilot last?

Long enough to measure with real data: typically 30 to 90 days with evaluation criteria defined from day one. A pilot with no evaluation date becomes permanent spending.

Can I switch vendors later?

Yes, as long as your data and integrations are documented from the start. Before signing, ask how your configuration and data get exported: the answer tells you how locked in you would be.
