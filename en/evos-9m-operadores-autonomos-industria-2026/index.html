Evos Came Out of Stealth With $9M: Agents That Learn by Watching Your Best Employees | inicIA

By inicIA · October 15, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On October 14, 2026, Evos (Evolutionary Operating System, Inc.) came out of stealth announcing $9 million in pre-seed funding, co-led by Uncork Capital and Field Ventures, with participation from Crosslink Capital, Utopia Capital Management, Sandwith Ventures, and angel investors. The New York-based company, led by founder and CEO Urav Shah, builds autonomous AI operators for the physical economy: agents that learn the trade by watching a company's most experienced people and then do the work themselves, inside the systems the business already uses. The eye-catching number: Evos says it passed $1 million in annual recurring revenue (ARR) just four weeks after going live.

Who it targets (and why that is smart)

Evos targets small and mid-sized businesses in physical industries — manufacturing, logistics, trucking — where software has been slowest to reach. Its numbers: 99.3% of U.S. motor carriers run fewer than 100 trucks and 93% of manufacturers have fewer than 100 employees. Each runs its own way, and its most valuable asset is not on the balance sheet: it is the judgment of its best people, built over decades and rarely written down. Those people are retiring faster than they can be replaced — and when they leave, the know-how walks out the door with them. The Evos operator learns the job from those people, improves on it, and proves it can match them before taking on work by itself.

The launch facts

  • Round: $9M pre-seed (Oct 14, 2026), co-led by Uncork Capital and Field Ventures.
  • Platform: "Workhorse," an agentic operations platform; operators learn by observing human behavior in real systems.
  • Human approval gate: AI decisions pass through human approval to ensure accuracy and oversight.
  • Early traction: $1M+ in ARR four weeks after launch; general availability planned for late 2026.
  • Early customer: MyFreightWorld processed hundreds of thousands of dollars in invoices on the platform.

The lesson for your business (even if you are not industrial)

  1. Undocumented knowledge is your at-risk asset: identify what your best employee knows that nobody else does — and that would leave with them. That is the first thing an agent should learn.
  2. Learning by observing beats programming rules: agents that watch how your people work capture judgment, not just steps. Demand that from your implementation.
  3. Human approval is not optional at the start: Evos makes it the entry gate. In your processes that move money, too.
  4. Measure traction, not demos: $1M in ARR in four weeks is the metric that separates a product from a pitch deck.
