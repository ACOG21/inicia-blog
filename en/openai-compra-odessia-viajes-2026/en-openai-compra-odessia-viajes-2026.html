OpenAI bought Odessia: travel enters ChatGPT's agenda

By inicIA · November 17, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

On November 10, 2026, Francis Davidson, founder of the AI travel startup Odessia and co-founder of Sonder, announced that OpenAI acquired Odessia and that he is joining OpenAI to lead travel technology development. The standalone service closed the same day, 27 days after opening to the public on October 14. Terms were not disclosed and OpenAI has not announced a travel product.

What Odessia was and what OpenAI bought

  • A travel concierge by text: Odessia opened to the public on October 14, 2026 as an always-on concierge, free, reached by text message. It was built on GPT-5.5 and Codex, and operated as a full-service agent: it planned, booked and managed changes, instead of redirecting the user to a supplier's website.
  • IATA accreditation and a team of eight: Odessia held IATA accreditation, which let it issue airline tickets and access private hotel rates. The team was eight people in San Francisco, with about $6 million in seed funding led by Sequoia Capital.
  • More than $60 million in planned travel: Davidson said tens of thousands of people used the service in the summer of 2026, with travel planned at a rate of more than $60 million a year. That figure measures planned travel volume, not Odessia's revenue.
  • A team purchase, not a business purchase: TechCrunch read it as an acqui-hire: OpenAI confirmed Davidson's hire and Odessia's site confirms the acquisition, but there is no OpenAI statement on the purchase and no product announced under the Odessia name.

What happened in the market on November 10

  • Travel shares fell: Expedia dropped nearly 4%, Tripadvisor more than 3% and Booking Holdings more than 2% on the day of the announcement, according to PYMNTS citing Seeking Alpha. The market read the purchase as a threat to established booking platforms.
  • United closed the door to unauthorized agents: a day later, United Airlines barred unauthorized AI agents from booking on its behalf. The airline draws the line: an agent books only if the airline authorizes it.
  • Travel is the test of the agent that sells: planning a trip is easy for a chatbot; selling it requires inventory, payment, ticket issuance and change management. Odessia had solved that hard part: accreditation, payment and changes. That is what OpenAI bought.
  • ChatGPT is already a travel channel: an OpenAI spokesperson told TechCrunch that trip planning is among the everyday tasks people bring to ChatGPT, and OpenAI already counts Booking.com, Expedia and Kayak among its customers or partners. The purchase adds the team that knows how to close the sale inside the chat.

What to do in your business this week

  • If you sell travel, check your chat channel: your customers already ask about trips in ChatGPT. The question is whether your inventory and your bookings reach that chat, or whether another agent sells in your place.
  • If your business sells through an agent, separate recommending from selling: recommending is content; selling is inventory, payment and changes. The agent that only recommends hands the sale away; the one that sells needs your systems connected.
  • Check who authorizes agents in your industry: United showed that the supplier can ban the agent. Before building a booking agent, confirm which platforms accept it and on what terms.
  • Do not wait for OpenAI's product to move: there is no date and no product announced. The useful move today is connecting your inventory and your booking to the channels where your customers already ask.

Note: this article summarizes Francis Davidson's announcement of November 10, 2026 and coverage by TechCrunch, Skift, PYMNTS, Unite.AI and Travel Market News. The deal terms were not disclosed; the $60 million figure measures planned travel, not revenue; the link between the share drop and the announcement is the market's reading that day.
