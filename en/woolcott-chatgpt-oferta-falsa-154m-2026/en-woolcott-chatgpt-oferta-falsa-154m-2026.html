He used ChatGPT to forge a £154M takeover bid: sentenced in London

By inicIA · November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer

Christopher Woolcott, 44, of Greenwich, London, was sentenced on November 10, 2026 at Southwark Crown Court over a fake takeover bid of £154 million for Touchstone Exploration Inc. Woolcott pleaded guilty on September 10, 2026 to one count of fraud by false representation and three counts of making a false instrument. The sentence: two years in prison suspended for two years, 200 hours of unpaid work and £4,627.73 in costs. The fake bid's documents were forged with ChatGPT. The case was investigated and prosecuted by the UK's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA).

How the fraud worked

  • He bought the shares first: Woolcott built a sizeable stake in Touchstone Exploration, an oil and gas company listed on London's AIM and the Toronto Stock Exchange. Then he launched the fake bid to push the share price up and sell at a profit. The FCA estimates his potential gain at up to £70,000.
  • Four false identities: to give the bid credibility, Woolcott invented several identities, including a fake private equity partner, a fake colleague and a fake lawyer. Judge Karu described the operation as deliberate, sophisticated and planned, with four false identities and forged documents.
  • Documents forged with ChatGPT: Woolcott forged the takeover documents with ChatGPT, including a fake bank letter. Coverage of the case reports an HSBC letter claiming £217 million. Touchstone ultimately determined that the bid was not legitimate.
  • He met the CEO under a false name: Woolcott met Touchstone's chief executive at a central London hotel under a false name, speaking with an American accent, to sustain the fiction in person.

How they caught him

  • The FCA opened the criminal investigation in March 2025: the authority combined transaction reporting data, phone records, receipts, CCTV footage and evidence seized from his home and workplace to identify Woolcott as the person behind the fraud.
  • Eighteen months to the guilty pleas: the FCA formally charged Woolcott 16 months after opening the case and secured the guilty pleas within 18 months of launching the investigation.
  • Touchstone was outside the investigation: the target company was not under FCA investigation in this case. The victim was the market: a fake bid that moves a price moves real money from real investors.
  • The full sentence: two years suspended for two years, with 200 hours of unpaid work and £4,627.73 in costs. The three 14-month custodial sentences for the forgery offences run concurrently with the suspended sentence.

What to do in your business this week

  • Verify through a channel you already have on file: the control that defeats this fraud is calling back a number already in your records. The number on the new document stays outside the verification. A good-looking document proves zero; a verified channel proves a lot.
  • Treat every large offer as suspect until verified: a takeover bid, a bank letter and a lawyer with a name and signature take minutes to fabricate today. Verification runs outside the package you received: official registry, the bank through its own channel and the lawyer through their bar association.
  • Write the protocol before you need it: define who verifies, through which channel and with what evidence before the first offer arrives. Fraud runs against the clock; a written protocol is what buys you time.
  • Train the team with this case: Woolcott's fraud is a real case with a real sentence, ideal for a 15-minute session: what he did, how they caught him and which control would have stopped him at first contact.

Note: this article summarizes the FCA press release of November 10, 2026 and coverage by Accountancy Daily, FinanceFeeds, AML Intelligence and Business Quarter. The £154M figure is the nominal value of the fake bid. The potential £70,000 gain is the FCA's estimate. The £217M bank letter is reported by case coverage, not by the FCA release.
