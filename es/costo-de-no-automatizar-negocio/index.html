Direct answer

When your team uses AI, your clients' data can end up in places you do not control: in a free assistant's chat, in a plugin with no contract, in a tool someone installed without permission. That is called shadow AI, and IBM's Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025 put a price on it: shadow AI-linked breaches cost an average of $4.63 million, about $670,000 more than a standard breach; 20% of organizations reported this kind of breach, and 97% of those affected lacked proper access controls for AI. Protecting your clients does not require being a cybersecurity expert: it requires four clear rules about which data goes into which tools, under what contract, and under what supervision.

Your data is already traveling: the shadow AI problem

The biggest risk is not the movie hacker: it is your own team pasting information into unapproved tools. The numbers:

  • 75% of knowledge workers use generative AI, and 78% of those users bring their own tools to work (Microsoft Work Trend Index 2024, 31,000 people across 31 countries).
  • 27.4% of corporate data employees put into AI tools was sensitive information, and the total volume of corporate data flowing into those tools grew 485% in one year (Cyberhaven analysis).
  • 63% of organizations affected by AI breaches had no AI governance policy (IBM 2025).

Translation for your business: if you do not provide an official tool and clear rules, your team is already using AI on its own, with your clients' data.

What happens to what gets pasted into a chatbot

When someone pastes a client's email, a contract, or a database into a free assistant, that text travels to the provider's servers. Depending on the plan and contract, it can be used to train models, remain in logs, or be accessible to third parties. With an enterprise plan under a data processing agreement, the provider commits to not training on your data and to processing it only to serve you.

The difference is not the tool: it is the contract. The same question asked in a personal chat and in an enterprise environment with a data agreement has two different destinations for your information.

The 4 rules to protect your clients

  1. One official list of approved tools. Everything that processes client data goes through this list. What is not on the list does not touch client data. Simple and auditable.
  2. No sensitive data in free tools. Names, emails, phones, documents, figures: if it identifies a client, it only goes into tools with an enterprise contract. This single rule removes most of the risk.
  3. A contract with every provider. A data processing agreement stating: no training on my data, processing only for the service, and deletion on request. If the provider will not sign, it is not a provider.
  4. Minimum access and logging. Each AI agent accesses only the data it needs for its task, and everything is logged: which data, which tool, when. If something fails, you know exactly where to look.

What to ask your AI provider

Before signing, ask in writing: where data is processed, whether it is used to train models, how long it is kept, who can access it, and how it is deleted when the contract ends. A serious provider answers this without dodging; one that evades is disqualified.

Also: agents serving your clients must operate with minimized data —the scheduling agent does not need to see your accounting— and with a human reviewing sensitive cases.

Frequently asked questions

Is using a free assistant with client data illegal?

Not automatically illegal, but it can violate your contractual and regulatory obligations depending on your industry (healthcare, finance, and legal have strict rules). The practical problem: without an enterprise contract, you do not control what happens to that data.

Does an enterprise AI plan solve everything?

It solves the contract part with the provider. The internal part remains: what data your employees input, with what permissions, and under what supervision. Contract + internal rules + logging: the three legs.

What is shadow AI in one sentence?

The use of AI tools without the business's approval or visibility. It is the most common risk because you do not see it until it has already happened.
